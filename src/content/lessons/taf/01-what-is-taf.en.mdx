## A forecast for an aerodrome

A <Term id="taf" /> (*terminal aerodrome forecast*) is the **weather forecast for an aerodrome**. While the <Term id="metar" /> tells you what the weather **is**, the TAF tells you what weather **is expected** during a given period within a few kilometres of the airport.

It is prepared by a forecaster at the responsible meteorological office and uses almost the same codes as the METAR, which makes it much easier to learn one after the other.

## What is it used for?

- Deciding whether to depart for a destination and whether **extra fuel** is needed.
- Choosing **alternate aerodromes** with suitable forecast conditions.
- Anticipating phenomena such as fog, thunderstorms or strong wind at the expected arrival time.

## Validity

Each TAF is valid for a specific period which, under ICAO rules, can be **no less than 6 hours and no more than 30**. In practice, many aerodromes publish 9-hour TAFs and major international airports 24- or 30-hour TAFs, updated periodically.

## What it includes and what it doesn't

| Includes | Does not (usually) include |
|---|---|
| Wind, visibility, forecast weather and cloud | RVR |
| Expected changes (FM, BECMG, TEMPO) | Dew point |
| Probabilities (PROB30, PROB40) | Pressure (QNH) |
| Maximum and minimum temperatures (TX/TN), when included | |

<Callout type="info" label="A forecast, not a certainty">
A TAF is a professional's best estimate, not a guarantee. That's why it is always read together with the latest METARs, which tell you whether the forecast is coming true.
</Callout>
