Detailed security procedures are restricted information and are set by each operator in its security programme. This lesson only summarises the framework and the public principles.
</Callout>

## Safety and security

In Spanish both are called *seguridad*, but in aviation they are different:

- **Safety**: preventing accidents caused by technical failures, errors or the environment.
- **Security**: protecting civil aviation against **deliberate acts** of <Term id="unlawful-interference">unlawful interference</Term>: hijackings, sabotage, attacks, bringing weapons on board or false threats.

## Who sets the rules

| Level | Instrument |
|---|---|
| Worldwide | ICAO **Annex 17**, *Security* |
| European Union | **Regulation (EC) 300/2008**, with the common basic standards, and **Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/1998**, with the detailed measures |
| State | National civil aviation security programme |
| Airport and airline | Each airport's and each operator's security programme |

Some of the detailed measures are not published: they are given only to those who need them, so as not to make them easier to evade.

## Screening

Before entering security-restricted areas and the aircraft, passengers, crews and staff go through <Term id="security-screening" />, as does their cabin baggage. Hold baggage is screened too.

EU rules group the **articles prohibited in the cabin** into categories:

1. **Guns, firearms** and other devices that discharge projectiles (including replicas).
2. **Stunning** devices.
3. Objects with a **sharp point or edge**.
4. **Workers' tools** that could be used as a weapon.
5. **Blunt** instruments.
6. **Explosives** and **incendiary** substances.

**Liquids** in cabin baggage are also restricted. The general EU rule is containers of **up to 100 ml** inside a **transparent one-litre bag**, although some airports with more modern detection equipment apply different conditions.

## Baggage reconciliation

One key measure: **a checked bag does not fly unless its passenger is on board**, unless it has gone through additional controls. If a checked-in passenger does not board, their baggage is found and offloaded, even if it delays the flight.

## The crew too

Crews go through screening, protect their ID and uniforms and stay alert throughout the service. Their role is threefold: **prevent**, **detect** anything unusual and **report** it to the right person.
