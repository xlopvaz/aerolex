## The four levels

ICAO uses a classification of **four threat levels** for incidents with <Term id="unruly-passenger">unruly passengers</Term>:

| Level | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Disruptive** behaviour: suspicious or verbally threatening |
| **2** | **Physically abusive** behaviour |
| **3** | **Life-threatening** behaviour |
| **4** | **Attempted or actual breach** of the flight crew compartment |

The level guides the response: the higher it is, the more the flight deck is protected and the firmer the measures. Pilot and controller associations (IFALPA and IFATCA) called in 2026 for all States to apply the system consistently.

## Alcohol

Alcohol is involved in many of these incidents. EU operating rules require the operator to take reasonable steps so that **nobody boards or remains on board** under the influence of alcohol or drugs to the extent that safety is endangered. In practice:

- an intoxicated passenger can be **denied boarding**;
- alcohol is **served responsibly** and service stops for anyone showing signs of intoxication;
- consumption of **passengers' own alcohol** brought on board is watched.

## De-escalating the conflict

Most incidents are resolved at level 1 if action is taken early:

- **stay calm** and keep a low, firm tone of voice;
- **listen** and acknowledge the problem ("I understand you're upset");
- **explain** the rule and its reasons, without arguing or humiliating;
- offer **options** where possible (another seat, a glass of water);
- **do not handle it alone**: inform the senior cabin crew member and, if it continues, the commander;
- **record** what happened: time, words, witnesses.

## What the Tokyo Convention says

The **1963 Tokyo Convention** governs offences and acts committed on board. Its main ideas:

- The **commander** may take **reasonable measures**, including **restraint**, against a person who has committed or is about to commit an act that endangers safety or good order on board.
- They may require or authorise the help of the **crew** and request that of **passengers**.
- Any crew member or passenger may take reasonable preventive measures **without authorisation** if they believe such action is immediately necessary to protect safety.
- The commander may **disembark** the person in any State where the aircraft lands and **deliver** them to the authorities if they believe a serious offence has been committed.
- People acting in accordance with the Convention are **protected** from liability for their actions.

## The 2014 Montreal Protocol

Under the Tokyo Convention, jurisdiction lay mainly with the State of **registration** of the aircraft, and many unruly passengers went unpunished when the aircraft landed in another country. The **2014 Montreal Protocol** extends jurisdiction to the **State of landing** and the **State of the operator**. According to ICAO, in April 2024 the Tokyo Convention had **187** States Parties and the Protocol **47**.
