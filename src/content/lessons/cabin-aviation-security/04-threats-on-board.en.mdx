<Callout type="official-procedure" label="The operator's manual prevails">
Detailed security procedures are restricted information and are set by each operator in its security programme. This lesson only summarises the framework and the public principles.
</Callout>

## Bomb threat

A threat may reach the airline by phone, come as a note found on board or as a passenger's remark. All are taken seriously:

1. The commander is **informed**, and coordinates the **threat assessment** with the ground.
2. If appropriate, the aircraft is **searched** using a **checklist**. ICAO Annex 6 requires the operator to carry on board a list of procedures for searching for a bomb and guidance on what to do if one is found, including the aircraft's **least-risk bomb location**, defined by the manufacturer.
3. The landing and a possible **evacuation** are prepared, as the commander decides.

## Suspicious item

If something suspicious is found:

- **do not touch it, move it or open it**;
- **move** passengers away from the area, if possible;
- inform the commander with a precise **description**: what it is, where it is, since when;
- follow the operator's procedure, which decides whether and how it is moved to the least-risk location.

## Unlawful seizure

In an attempt to **seize** the aircraft (a hijacking), the detailed procedures are confidential, but the public principles are clear:

- people's **lives** come first;
- the <Term id="flight-deck-door" /> stays **closed**, as Annex 6 provides;
- the cabin crew **discreetly alert** the pilots;
- the pilots can indicate the situation to ATC with <Term id="squawk" /> **7500**;
- **observe** and remember as much as possible (number of people, appearance, weapons, language) for the authorities.

## Afterwards

Every security occurrence is **reported** and **recorded**. Reports help the authorities and the airline improve their measures, and are part of the security system just like the checks.
