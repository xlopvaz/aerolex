## Checking the aircraft before flight

Before boarding, checks are made to ensure that nobody has left **prohibited items** on the aircraft and that there are no unauthorised people. EU rules set out when an aircraft **security check** or a full **security search** is needed. In practice, cabin crew check the areas they are responsible for following the operator's list:

- **lavatories**;
- overhead **bins** and stowages;
- seat pockets and under the seats;
- **galleys** and containers;
- crew rest areas.

Any suspicious item is **not touched**: the commander is informed and the procedure is followed.

During the turnaround, the aircraft must be **protected** against unauthorised access: unused doors are closed and access is controlled.

## The flight deck door

After the 2001 attacks, ICAO rules reinforced the <Term id="flight-deck-door" />. Annex 6 provides, among other things, that on larger passenger aircraft:

- the door must **resist** penetration by small-arms fire and grenade shrapnel, and **forcible entry**;
- it must be possible to **lock and unlock** it from either pilot's station;
- it must stay **locked** from the time all external doors are closed after boarding until one is opened for disembarkation, except for access by authorised persons;
- there must be a means of **monitoring** the area outside the door from the flight deck;
- and the cabin crew must be able to **discreetly alert** the pilots to suspicious activity.

Each operator has an **access procedure**: how entry is requested, how the caller is identified and what to do with the area in front of the door while it is open.

## Reporting unlawful interference

- **On board**: the cabin crew use the agreed **discreet** means to alert the flight deck, without alerting the person making the threat.
- **To ATC**: <Term id="transponder" /> code **7500** indicates that the aircraft is subject to unlawful interference. Controllers recognise it and act accordingly without the pilots needing to say it on the radio.

<Callout type="caution" label="No improvising">
In a real threat, the rule is to apply the trained procedure, put people's lives first and not open the flight deck except as the procedure says.
</Callout>
