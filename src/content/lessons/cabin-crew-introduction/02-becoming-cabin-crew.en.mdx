Each airline can add its own requirements to those in the regulations (for example, languages or being able to swim) and publishes them in its recruitment process.
</Callout>

## The cabin crew attestation

To work as cabin crew in commercial air transport in the EU you need a <Term id="cabin-crew-attestation">cabin crew attestation (CCA)</Term>, governed by Part-CC of Regulation (EU) No 1178/2011. The steps are:

| Step | What the rules require |
|---|---|
| Age | Be at least **18 years old** |
| Initial training | Pass an **initial training course** and its examination, given by an approved organisation |
| Medical fitness | Pass an **aero-medical assessment** before first being assigned to duties |
| Attestation | With all of the above, the attestation is issued; it stays valid as long as the holder keeps working |

The attestation stops being valid if its holder **has not exercised its privileges for 60 months** on at least one aircraft type.

## Then, the operator's training

The attestation is only the way in. Before flying with an airline, cabin crew receive training from that operator and for each aircraft type:

- **Operator conversion:** the airline's procedures, equipment and safety culture.
- **Aircraft type training:** the doors, exits, equipment and systems of each model.
- **Differences training:** when the variant or equipment of a known aircraft changes.
- **Familiarisation flights:** the first flights under supervision.

## Staying current

- **Recurrent training:** every cabin crew member completes training and checking **every year**, including, for example, practice in operating each type of door and exit.
- **Refresher training:** someone who has not flown on a type for a while reviews it before returning.
- **Aero-medical assessment:** repeated at intervals of **60 months at most**, or sooner if the doctor says so.
- Crew resource management (<Term id="crm" />) training runs through the whole process.

<Callout type="info" label="In Spain">
The cabin crew attestation follows the same European rules. Check the current requirements and procedures on AESA's website before you start.
</Callout>
