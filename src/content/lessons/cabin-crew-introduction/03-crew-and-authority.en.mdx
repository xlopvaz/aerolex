The calls, signals and keywords used between the cabin and the flight deck differ from airline to airline. Always follow those in your manual.
</Callout>

## Who is in charge on board

The **commander** is the highest authority on board and has final responsibility for the safety of the aircraft, its occupants and its cargo. Everyone on board must follow the commander's lawful instructions given for the safety of the flight.

Below that, the chain is simple:

1. **Commander** (and, if absent or incapacitated, the next pilot as set out in the manual).
2. <Term id="senior-cabin-crew-member">Senior cabin crew member</Term>: responsible to the commander for coordinating the cabin.
3. **Cabin crew members**: each with their own doors, zone and assigned tasks.

## The senior cabin crew member

- Holds the **pre-flight briefing** with the cabin crew: positions, equipment, service and anything special about the flight.
- Receives information from the commander (flight time, weather, expected turbulence, special situations) and passes it on.
- Coordinates normal and emergency procedures in the cabin and reports any incident to the commander.

## Communicating with the flight deck

Communication is normally by **interphone**, using agreed procedures: normal calls, priority calls and emergency calls, each with its own signal. Some general principles:

- Messages that are **short, clear and complete**: what is happening, where, and what has been done.
- **Confirm** what you receive to avoid misunderstandings.
- Tell the <Term id="flight-deck" /> about **anything** that might affect safety, even if it seems minor: a strange smell, a noise, a passenger behaving worryingly.

## The sterile flight deck

During taxi, take-off, landing and, generally, below an altitude set by the operator (in the US, below 10,000 ft except in cruise), the <Term id="sterile-flight-deck" /> rule applies: the pilots deal only with matters essential to the flight. During that time cabin crew **call only** for safety matters.
