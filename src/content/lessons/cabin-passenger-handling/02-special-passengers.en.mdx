<Callout type="official-procedure" label="The operator's manual prevails">
EU rules set the framework; the details (which passengers may sit in each row, how boarding works, what is offered in a delay) are set out in each airline's manual.
</Callout>

## Persons with reduced mobility

**Regulation (EC) 1107/2006** protects <Term id="prm">disabled persons and persons with reduced mobility</Term> travelling by air in the EU:

- an airline **may not refuse** their booking or boarding because of their disability, except for justified **safety reasons** established by law;
- **assistance** at the airport (from arriving at the terminal to the seat, and back) is provided by the **airport managing body** and is **free of charge**;
- to guarantee it, the need must be **notified at least 48 hours** before departure.

On board, the crew helps with baggage, an adapted safety briefing and moving about the cabin, following the operator's procedure.

### Communicating well

- Speak **directly** to the person, not to their companion.
- **Ask** before helping, and how they would like to be helped.
- With blind people, **introduce yourself** and describe the surroundings; with deaf people, face them and speak clearly.
- Give the **safety briefing** individually if needed, including the nearest exit.

## Unaccompanied minors

An <Term id="unaccompanied-minor" /> travels in the care of the airline, under the age and service conditions each airline sets. Usually:

- a **formal handover** of the child by an adult at departure and to an identified adult at arrival, with signed paperwork;
- a cabin crew member in charge during the flight;
- a seat where the crew can look after them and that is **not** next to an emergency exit.

## Passengers who are ill or have medical needs

A passenger with a recent illness, who needs oxygen or a stretcher, or whose condition could worsen in flight, may need prior **medical clearance** from the airline. If a passenger looks clearly ill at boarding, the crew member reports it: the commander has the final say on carrying them.

## Passengers in custody

Some passengers travel **escorted by officers** (for example, on a deportation). The airline sets the conditions: the commander must be informed, they are seated away from exits and are generally served without alcohol or metal cutlery.
