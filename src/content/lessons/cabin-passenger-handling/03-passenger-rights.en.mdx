<Callout type="official-procedure" label="The operator's manual prevails">
EU rules set the framework; the details (which passengers may sit in each row, how boarding works, what is offered in a delay) are set out in each airline's manual.
</Callout>

## Who it applies to

**Regulation (EC) 261/2004** applies to passengers departing from an EU airport, and to those arriving in the EU from outside on an EU airline. It covers three situations: <Term id="denied-boarding" />, **cancellation** and **long delay**.

## The three kinds of rights

| Right | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Compensation** | A fixed amount depending on flight distance |
| **Care** | Food and drink, two calls or messages and, if an overnight stay is needed, a hotel and transport |
| **Refund or re-routing** | Refunding the ticket or getting the passenger to their destination another way |

### Compensation

| Flight distance | Compensation |
|---|---|
| Up to 1,500 km | **€250** |
| Intra-EU flights over 1,500 km, and others of 1,500 to 3,500 km | **€400** |
| Over 3,500 km | **€600** |

## Denied boarding

If there are more passengers than seats, the airline must first ask for **volunteers** to give up their seats in exchange for agreed benefits. If there are not enough and it denies boarding to someone against their will, it owes them compensation, care and a refund or re-routing. It does not count as denied boarding when there are **reasonable grounds**, such as safety or inadequate travel documents.

## Cancellation

Compensation is due unless the passenger was informed early enough (for example, **at least two weeks** before) or the cancellation was caused by **extraordinary circumstances** that could not have been avoided, such as certain weather conditions or security risks.

## Long delay

The regulation gives a right to **care** after a certain delay, which depends on distance (2, 3 or 4 hours), and to a **refund** if the delay reaches **5 hours** and the passenger decides not to travel. In addition, the **EU Court of Justice** ruled in 2009 (the Sturgeon case) that passengers who reach their destination **3 hours or more** late are entitled to the same compensation as for a cancellation, unless there were extraordinary circumstances.

## The crew's role

Cabin crew do not decide on compensation, but they greatly influence how a delay is experienced:

- **inform** frequently, with real information and without speculating;
- offer the **care** the operator has planned on board (water, food);
- **do not promise** compensation or amounts: refer passengers to the airline's channels;
- stay **calm** and empathetic, because frustration can turn into an incident.

<Callout type="info" label="Dialogue · Diálogo">
**Passenger:** We've been sitting here an hour. Are you going to pay me anything?

**Cabin crew:** I understand you're upset. We're waiting for departure clearance because of the storm; the captain will give us a time as soon as he has one. Information on compensation rights is on the airline's website and at the airport. Can I bring you a glass of water?
