<Callout type="official-procedure" label="The operator's manual prevails">
EU rules set the framework; the details (which passengers may sit in each row, how boarding works, what is offered in a delay) are set out in each airline's manual.
</Callout>

## Boarding

During <Term id="boarding" />, the cabin crew:

- welcome and **observe** each passenger: someone who looks drunk, ill or very nervous, someone who needs help, a suspicious bag;
- help people find their seats and **stow** their baggage;
- at the end, check that the **number of passengers** matches the flight documents.

## Cabin baggage

EU operating rules require cabin baggage to be **stowed** so that it cannot move or injure anyone and does **not block** aisles, exits or emergency equipment. In practice:

- under the seat in front or in the **overhead bins**, never at the feet in exit rows;
- with the bins **closed** for take-off and landing;
- heavy items low down; oversized bags are sent to the hold.

## Who sits by the exits

The operator must seat passengers so that they **help rather than hinder** an evacuation. That is why seats next to <Term id="emergency-exit">emergency exits</Term> are not given to people who might struggle to open them or slow others down, for example:

- <Term id="prm">persons with reduced mobility</Term>;
- children, infants and those travelling with them;
- people travelling in custody;
- passengers travelling with animals;
- people who do not understand the instructions or cannot act quickly.

Passengers in those rows get an **additional briefing** on how to open the exit and must agree to help if needed.

## Infants

In the EU, an infant may travel on an adult's lap, held by a **supplementary loop belt** attached to the adult's belt, or in an approved **child restraint system** in their own seat. Each adult holds only one infant, and the number of infants per row is limited by the number of oxygen masks.

## The safety briefing

Before take-off, passengers are given the safety briefing: seat belts, exits, floor-level emergency lighting, baggage, electronic devices, safety card and, where relevant, oxygen and life jackets. The <Term id="safety-demonstration" /> is given by cabin crew or shown on video.

## Refuelling with passengers on board

EU rules allow refuelling while passengers are boarding, on board or disembarking, under conditions. Among them:

- continuous **communication** between the ground staff supervising refuelling and the crew;
- crew ready for an immediate **evacuation**, with exits clear;
- **no smoking** sign on and seat belts **unfastened**.
