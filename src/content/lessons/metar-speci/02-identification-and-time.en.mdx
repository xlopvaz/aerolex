## The ICAO location indicator

After the report type (`METAR` or `SPECI`) comes the **ICAO location indicator**: four letters that identify the aerodrome. It is not the three-letter code on your boarding pass (that is the IATA code).

| Airport | ICAO | IATA |
|---|---|---|
| Madrid-Barajas | `LEMD` | MAD |
| Barcelona-El Prat | `LEBL` | BCN |
| Santiago-Rosalía de Castro | `LEST` | SCQ |
| Vigo | `LEVX` | VGO |
| A Coruña | `LECO` | LCG |
| London Heathrow | `EGLL` | LHR |
| Paris-Charles de Gaulle | `LFPG` | CDG |
| New York JFK | `KJFK` | JFK |

The **first letter** indicates a large region and the first two usually identify the country: `LE` is Spain, `LF` France, `EG` the United Kingdom, `ED` Germany, `LP` Portugal. In the contiguous United States indicators start with `K`.

## Day and time: `DDHHMMZ`

The date-time group has six digits and a `Z`:

- `DD`: day of the month.
- `HHMM`: hours and minutes of the observation.
- `Z`: the time is in UTC (Zulu).

The month and year are **not included**: they are implied.

<MetarBreakdown parts={[
  ["METAR", "Routine report"],
  ["LEMD", "Madrid-Barajas"],
  ["121830Z", "Day 12, at 18:30 UTC"]
]} />

In winter, mainland Spain is on UTC+1, so `121830Z` is 19:30 local time; in summer (UTC+2) it is 20:30. The Canary Islands are one hour behind the mainland.

## Modifiers: AUTO, COR and NIL

| Code | Where it appears | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `AUTO` | After the time | Fully automated report, with no human intervention |
| `COR` | After the report type | Corrected report: it replaces an earlier one that contained an error |
| `NIL` | Instead of the rest of the report | Missing report: no data |

<Callout type="tip" label="Tip">
Always check **the time** before reading the rest: a METAR from two hours ago may not reflect the current weather. If there is a later SPECI, that one applies.
</Callout>
