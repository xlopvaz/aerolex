## An airport's weather in a single line

A <Term id="metar" /> is the **routine weather report for an aerodrome**. It is produced by the airport's meteorological office or by an automatic station and describes the weather **right now**: wind, visibility, phenomena such as rain or fog, cloud, temperature and pressure.

It is published at regular intervals, usually **every 30 or 60 minutes**, in a code standardised internationally by ICAO (Annex 3) and the World Meteorological Organization (Manual on Codes, WMO-No. 306). That is why a pilot can read a METAR from Madrid exactly like one from Tokyo.

<MetarBreakdown parts={[

  ["121830Z", "Day 12 of the month, 18:30 UTC"],

  ["9999", "Visibility 10 km or more"],
  ["SCT030", "Scattered cloud at 3,000 ft"],

Don't worry if you don't understand every group yet: the next lessons go through them one by one.

## And what is a SPECI?

A <Term id="speci" /> is a **special report**. It has exactly the same format as a METAR, but it is issued **between two METARs** when the weather changes significantly: for example, when a thunderstorm begins, when visibility drops below certain thresholds or when the cloud base comes down. Nobody has to wait for the next routine report to learn about an important deterioration.

The exact criteria that trigger a SPECI are set by each State's meteorological authority, following ICAO Annex 3.

## Who uses them?

- **Pilots and dispatchers**, to plan the flight, decide whether a take-off or landing is possible and choose alternate aerodromes.
- **Air traffic controllers**, who use the data to prepare the information broadcast to crews, such as the ATIS.
- **Enthusiasts and students**, because they are one of the best ways to understand real aviation weather.

<Callout type="info" label="METAR vs TAF">
A METAR describes the **observed** weather. To know what weather is **expected** in the coming hours you use the <Term id="taf" />, which has its own course.
</Callout>

## An order that never changes

The groups of a METAR **always appear in the same order**. If an element has nothing to report (for example, it isn't raining), its group is simply **left out**.

| Order | Group | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Report type | `METAR` / `SPECI` |
| 2 | ICAO location indicator | `LEMD` |
| 3 | Day and time (UTC) | `121830Z` |
| 4 | Wind | `24012KT` |
| 5 | Visibility and, when applicable, RVR | `9999` · `R35/1100U` |
| 6 | Present weather | `-RA` |
| 7 | Cloud (or CAVOK) | `SCT030` |
| 8 | Temperature / dew point | `18/10` |
| 9 | Pressure (QNH) | `Q1013` |
| 10 | Supplementary information | `RERA` · `WS R24` |
| 11 | Trend forecast | `NOSIG` |

<Callout type="regional-diff" label="ICAO / US">
US METARs follow the same general order but use some different units (statute miles, inches of mercury), do not include a trend and usually end with a remarks section (`RMK`). We'll point them out throughout the course.
</Callout>

All times in a METAR are in <Term id="utc" />, aviation's single clock.
