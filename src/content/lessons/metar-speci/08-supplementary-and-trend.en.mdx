## Recent weather: `RE`

It shows significant phenomena **observed since the previous report (within the last hour at most)** that are no longer present at the time of observation. They are written with `RE` in front:

- `RERA`: recent rain.
- `RETSRA`: recent thunderstorm with rain.
- `RESN`: recent snow.

It is useful, for example, to know the runway may be wet even though it has stopped raining.

## Wind shear: `WS`

If <Term id="wind-shear" /> has been reported in the take-off or approach paths, the affected runway (or all runways) is indicated:

## Trend forecast

At many European airports, the METAR ends with a **trend forecast** valid for the **2 hours** after the observation:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `NOSIG` | No significant change expected |
| `BECMG` | Becoming: a change expected to be reached and to persist |
| `TEMPO` | Temporary fluctuations, each lasting less than an hour |
| `FM` / `TL` / `AT` + time | From / until / at (HHMM UTC) |
| `NSW` | End of significant weather (*no significant weather*) |

<MetarBreakdown parts={[
  ["BECMG", "Becoming…"],
  ["FM1900", "…from 19:00 UTC:"],
  ["25015KT", "wind 250° at 15 knots"],
  ["TEMPO", "Temporarily:"],
  ["3000", "visibility 3,000 m"],
  ["SHRA", "in rain showers"]
]} />

The same indicators are used in the <Term id="taf" />, over longer periods.

<Callout type="regional-diff" label="ICAO / US">
US METARs have no trend. Instead they end with `RMK` (remarks), a section of additional observations. For example: `AO2` (automated station with a precipitation discriminator), `SLP199` (sea-level pressure 1019.9 hPa) or `T00831028` (temperature 8.3 °C and dew point −2.8 °C, in tenths).
</Callout>
