## The 60 % rule

To **dispatch** a flight, EU rules require the aircraft, at its planned landing mass, to be able to stop from **50 ft above the threshold**:

- for **jets**, within **60 %** of the <Term id="lda" />;
- for **turboprops**, within **70 %**.

Put the other way round: the LDA must be at least **1.67 times** the certified landing distance of a jet. If the runway is expected to be **wet**, the LDA must also be at least **115 %** of that required distance, i.e. about **1.92 times** the certified distance.

The margin is large because the certified distance comes from tests with test pilots, a dry runway and maximum braking, which line flying does not always reproduce.

### Example

Certified dry landing distance of 1,500 m:

- Dry: 1,500 / 0.6 = **2,500 m** of LDA required.
- Wet: 2,500 × 1.15 = **2,875 m**.

## The reference speed

The reference speed on final approach (VREF) is not less than **1.23 times the reference stall speed** in the landing configuration. Every extra knot lengthens the landing; flying fast or touching down late is a classic cause of **runway excursions**.

## What lengthens the landing

- High **mass**.
- A **tailwind**.
- High **density altitude**: true airspeed is higher for the same indicated airspeed.
- A **downhill** runway.
- A **wet or contaminated** runway.
- Arriving **high or fast** at the threshold.

<Term id="autobrake" />, <Term id="thrust-reverser" />s and <Term id="anti-skid" /> shorten it, although the dry dispatch distance does not count on reversers.

## Aquaplaning

With water on the runway, the tyres can lose contact with the surface. According to the FAA, dynamic aquaplaning can begin at about

> 9 × √(tyre pressure in psi) knots

A tyre at 200 psi: 9 × 14.1 ≈ **127 kt**. Once started, it can persist at lower speeds.

## Runway condition

Since November 2021 the ICAO **Global Reporting Format** has applied. The aerodrome assesses each **third** of the runway and assigns it an <Term id="rwycc" />:

| Code | Typical surface |
|---|---|
| **6** | Dry |
| **5** | Damp or frost; wet up to 3 mm |
| **4** | Very cold compacted snow |
| **3** | Wet and slippery, dry or wet snow |
| **2** | More than 3 mm of water or slush |
| **1** | Ice |
| **0** | Wet ice or water on compacted snow: extremely slippery |

It is reported, for example, as **5/5/3**, from threshold to end. For performance, the **lowest code** is used.

<Callout type="caution" label="Calculate again before landing">
Conditions change during the flight. Before the approach, the crew recalculates the landing distance with the actual wind, runway condition and configuration, even if the dispatch calculation was correct.
</Callout>
