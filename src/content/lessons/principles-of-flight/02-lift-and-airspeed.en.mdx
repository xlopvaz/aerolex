## The lift equation

The <Term id="lift" /> a wing produces is summed up in one equation:

**L = C<sub>L</sub> · ½ · ρ · V² · S**

| Term | What it is | Who controls it |
|---|---|---|
| C<sub>L</sub> | Lift coefficient | The pilot, through angle of attack and flaps |
| ρ (rho) | Air density | The atmosphere (altitude, temperature, humidity) |
| V | True airspeed | The pilot, with power and attitude |
| S | Wing area | The design (and flaps that extend it) |

The product **½ρV²** is the **dynamic pressure**: the "push" of the air reaching the wing. Because speed is squared, doubling it gives four times the lift.

## Lift coefficient and angle of attack

For a given airfoil, C<sub>L</sub> rises almost in a straight line with <Term id="angle-of-attack" /> up to a maximum, **C<sub>L</sub> max**, reached at the critical angle of attack. Beyond it the flow separates and the <Term id="stall" /> follows.

This leads to a key idea: to keep the same lift (equal to weight in level flight), **if speed goes down, angle of attack must go up**. Flying slowly means flying at a high angle of attack, close to C<sub>L</sub> max.

## Four different airspeeds

| Speed | What it is |
|---|---|
| <Term id="ias">IAS</Term> | Indicated: what the airspeed indicator shows, uncorrected |
| **CAS** | Calibrated: IAS corrected for instrument and position (pressure-port) errors |
| <Term id="tas">TAS</Term> | True: CAS corrected for altitude and temperature (and, at high speed, compressibility) |
| **GS** | Ground speed: TAS plus or minus the effect of the wind |

The airspeed indicator measures dynamic pressure, which depends on both ρ and V. That is why **at the same IAS the wing behaves the same at any altitude**, even though TAS is much higher up high. It is also why many limits and stall speeds are published as indicated airspeeds.

## The airspeed indicator arcs

On many light aircraft the airspeed indicator has colour markings:

- **White arc:** flap operating range. Its lower end is the stall speed with flaps and gear down (V<sub>S0</sub>) and its upper end the maximum flap-extended speed (V<sub>FE</sub>).
- **Green arc:** normal operating range, from the clean stall speed (V<sub>S1</sub>) to the maximum structural cruising speed (V<sub>NO</sub>).
- **Yellow arc:** caution range, smooth air only.
- **Red line:** never-exceed speed (V<sub>NE</sub>).
