## Static and dynamic

- **Static stability** is the **initial tendency** after a disturbance. It is positive if the aircraft tends to return to its original state, neutral if it stays in the new one and negative if it moves further away.
- **Dynamic stability** is what happens **over time**: whether the oscillations die out (positive), continue (neutral) or grow (negative).

An aircraft can be statically stable and still oscillate a lot: ideally both are positive, but not excessive, because an overly stable aircraft is hard to manoeuvre.

## Stability about the three axes

| Axis | Stability | What provides it |
|---|---|---|
| Lateral (pitch) | **Longitudinal** | The CG ahead of the centre of pressure and the horizontal stabilizer of the <Term id="empennage" /> |
| Longitudinal (roll) | **Lateral** | Wing dihedral, sweepback and keel effect (high wing) |
| Vertical (yaw) | **Directional** | The fin and the side area behind the CG, which act like a weathervane |

In the classic layout, the CG is **ahead** of the point where lift acts, so the aircraft tends to pitch nose-down. The horizontal stabilizer balances this by pushing the tail **down**. If a gust pitches the aircraft up, the stabilizer's angle of attack changes and creates a moment that brings the nose back.

Lateral and directional stability interact. If directional stability strongly outweighs lateral stability, **spiral instability** appears; if lateral dominates, **Dutch roll** — a combined rolling and yawing oscillation.

## The centre of gravity

Each aircraft's manual sets **forward and aft CG limits**. Outside them, the aircraft may not be safely controllable.

| Forward CG | Aft CG |
|---|---|
| More stable and heavier on the controls | Less stable and more sensitive |
| The tail must push down harder: like carrying more weight | The tail pushes less: slightly less drag |
| **Higher** stall speed | Lower stall speed |
| There may not be enough <Term id="elevator" /> for the flare | Recovery from a <Term id="stall" /> or spin may be difficult or impossible |

<Callout type="caution" label="Weight and balance">
Working out weight and balance before every flight is not a formality: a CG outside limits changes the aircraft's behaviour dangerously. The actual calculation methods are in each aircraft's manual.
</Callout>
