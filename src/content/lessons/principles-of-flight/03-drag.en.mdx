## Two big families

Total <Term id="drag" /> is the sum of two types that behave in opposite ways.

### Parasite drag

It is produced by any object moving through the air, whether or not it makes lift. It has three components:

- **Form drag:** depends on shape and frontal area (extended gear, an antenna, an engine).
- **Interference drag:** appears where two surfaces meet, such as the wing and fuselage, because the airflows mix.
- **Skin friction drag:** the friction of the air against the surface; dirt, ice or protruding rivets increase it.

Parasite drag **increases with the square of airspeed**: twice the speed, four times the drag.

### Induced drag

<Term id="induced-drag">Induced drag</Term> is the "price" of lift. Because pressure is higher below the wing than above it, air tends to curl around the tips from bottom to top and forms **wingtip vortices**. These vortices tilt the airflow behind the wing downward (downwash) and, with it, tilt the lift rearward: that rearward component is induced drag.

It is greatest when the wing works at a high angle of attack — that is, at **low speed** — and falls roughly with the square of airspeed. It also grows with **weight**, because more lift is needed.

It is reduced by wings with a high <Term id="aspect-ratio" /> (long and slender) and by wingtip devices such as <Term id="winglet">winglets</Term>.

## The total drag curve

Add the two and you get a U-shaped curve:

<MetarBreakdown kind="summary" parts={[
  ["Low speed", "Induced drag dominates: total drag is high"],
  ["L/D max speed", "Induced = parasite: minimum total drag and best lift-to-drag ratio"],
  ["High speed", "Parasite drag dominates: total drag rises again"]
]} caption="How total drag changes with airspeed." />

The **L/D max** speed matters because the aircraft is then flying with the least possible drag for its weight. On a propeller aircraft, for example, it is linked to the best glide speed and the maximum range speed.

<Callout type="tip" label="The back side of the curve">
Below the minimum drag speed, flying slower requires **more** thrust, not less. This is the **region of reversed command** (the "back side" of the curve), where an aircraft flies, for example, during a slow approach.
</Callout>
