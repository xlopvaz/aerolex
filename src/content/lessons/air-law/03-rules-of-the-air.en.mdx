## Right of way

The <Term id="right-of-way" /> rules remove doubt when two aircraft approach each other. The aircraft with right of way keeps its **heading and speed**; the one that must give way avoids passing ahead of, below or above it unless well clear.

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Head-on** | Both turn **right** |
| **Converging** at the same level | The one that has the other **on its right** gives way |
| **Overtaking** | The aircraft being overtaken has right of way; the overtaking one passes to the **right** |
| **On approach** | Aircraft in flight have priority over those on the ground, and the **lower** of two approaching aircraft has priority |

In addition, when aircraft of different types converge, the more manoeuvrable give way to the less: power-driven aeroplanes give way to airships, gliders and balloons; airships to gliders and balloons; gliders to balloons.

## VMC minima

The European rules (SERA) <Term id="vmc" /> (visual meteorological conditions) are:

| Where | Visibility | Distance from cloud |
|---|---|---|
| Classes A to G, **at or above FL100** | **8 km** | 1,500 m horizontally, 1,000 ft vertically |
| Classes A to G, **below FL100** and above 3,000 ft AMSL or 1,000 ft above terrain | **5 km** | 1,500 m horizontally, 1,000 ft vertically |
| Classes F and G, **at or below 3,000 ft AMSL**, or 1,000 ft above terrain or less | **5 km** | **Clear of cloud** and with the surface in sight |

In that lowest band, States may allow lower visibilities in some cases, for example at low speed. To take off, land or enter the circuit of an aerodrome in a control zone, the general rule also requires a ceiling of at least **1,500 ft** and a ground visibility of at least **5 km**.

## Minimum heights

- **VFR** over cities or settlements: at least **1,000 ft** above the highest obstacle within **600 m**.
- **VFR** elsewhere: at least **500 ft** above the ground or water.
- **IFR**: at least **1,000 ft** above the highest obstacle within **8 km**, or **2,000 ft** over high or mountainous terrain.

The exceptions are take-off and landing, and whatever the authority permits.

## Tower light signals

If the radio fails, the tower can communicate with light signals:

| Signal | In flight | On the ground |
|---|---|---|
| **Steady green** | Cleared to land | Cleared for take-off |
| **Steady red** | Give way and continue circling | Stop |
| **Flashing green** | Return for landing | Cleared to taxi |
| **Flashing red** | Aerodrome unsafe: do not land | Taxi clear of landing area |
| **Flashing white** | Land here and proceed to apron | Return to starting point |

## Interception

If a military aircraft intercepts a civil aircraft, the intercepted pilot must **follow the interceptor's instructions**, interpreting its visual signals, try to make contact on the emergency frequency **121.5 MHz** and select transponder code **7700**, unless otherwise instructed. The interceptor's basic signal, "follow me", is **rocking the wings**; the intercepted aircraft replies by rocking its wings too and following.
