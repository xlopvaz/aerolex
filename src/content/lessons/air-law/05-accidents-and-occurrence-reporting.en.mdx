## Three words with precise meanings

ICAO Annex 13 defines:

- **Accident**: an occurrence, between boarding and disembarkation, in which a person is **fatally or seriously injured**, the aircraft suffers **substantial damage** affecting its structure or performance, or the aircraft is **missing** or completely inaccessible.
- <Term id="serious-incident" />: an incident in which **there was a high probability** of an accident. For example, a near collision requiring an avoidance manoeuvre, a take-off rejected from a closed runway or a fire on board.
- **Incident**: any other occurrence that affects or could affect safety.

## Why investigate

Annex 13 is clear: the sole objective of investigating an accident or incident is to **prevent** future accidents. It is **not** to apportion blame or liability. That is why the safety investigation is **independent** of judicial investigations and sanctions.

In the EU, **Regulation (EU) 996/2010** requires each State to have an independent safety **investigation authority** (in Spain, the CIAIAC for civil aviation), which investigates all accidents and serious incidents. Its reports end with **safety recommendations** addressed to whoever can fix the problem.

## Reporting occurrences

Accidents are the end of a chain. Before them there are usually many minor occurrences that warn of the danger. **Regulation (EU) 376/2014** organises their reporting:

- **Mandatory reporting**: pilots, cabin crew, controllers, maintenance engineers and other professionals must report occurrences on a defined list **within 72 hours** of becoming aware of them.
- **Voluntary reporting**: any other occurrence the professional believes poses a risk.
- Organisations **analyse** the reports and take action, and the data feed a European database.

## Just culture

Nobody reports their own mistakes if they fear being punished. That is why the rules protect reporters within a <Term id="just-culture" />:

- they are not penalised for actions, omissions or decisions in line with their experience and training;
- the **confidentiality** of their identity is protected;
- **gross negligence**, **wilful** violations and **destructive** acts are **not** tolerated.

<Callout type="tip" label="Why it works">
Aviation is so safe largely because it learns from mistakes without waiting for them to cause an accident. A report about today's scare can prevent tomorrow's accident.
</Callout>
