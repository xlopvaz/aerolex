## The pyramid of rules

In the EU, aviation is regulated on several levels:

1. The **Basic Regulation** (EU) 2018/1139, setting the essential requirements and the powers of <Term id="easa" />.
2. The Commission's **implementing and delegated regulations**, binding in all Member States, with their "Parts" (Part-FCL, Part-ORO…).
3. The <Term id="amc" /> (acceptable means of compliance) and **guidance material** (GM) published by EASA. They are not binding, but anyone who follows an AMC **is presumed to comply**; an alternative means needs the authority's approval.

## Key regulations for a pilot

| Regulation | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **(EU) 1178/2011** (*Aircrew*) | Licences (Part-FCL), medical certificates (Part-MED), training organisations |
| **(EU) 965/2012** (*Air Operations*) | Flight operations: commercial air transport (Part-CAT), operator requirements (Part-ORO) and specific approvals (Part-SPA) |
| **(EU) 923/2012** (<Term id="sera" />) | The common rules of the air |
| **(EU) 1321/2014** | Continuing airworthiness |

## Aeroplane licences

| Licence | For | Minimum age |
|---|---|---|
| **LAPL** | Light aircraft licence, private flying in light aircraft | 17 |
| **PPL** | Private pilot, without pay | 17 |
| **CPL** | Commercial pilot: may be paid and act as commander of single-pilot aircraft | 18 |
| **ATPL** | Airline transport pilot: commander of multi-pilot aircraft | 21 |

The aeroplane ATPL requires, among other things, **1,500 hours** of flight time. Licences are completed with **ratings**: type ratings (for each complex aircraft), class ratings and the instrument rating (IR).

## Age limits in commercial air transport

- Between **60 and 64**, a pilot may only fly commercial air transport in **multi-pilot** crews, and only if the other pilot is under 60.
- From **65**, they may not act as a pilot in commercial air transport.

## The medical certificate

Professional licences require a **Class 1 medical certificate**; the PPL a **Class 2**. A Class 1 certificate is valid for **12 months**, reduced to **6 months** in some cases, for example from age 60.

<Callout type="info" label="Why so many Parts">
Each set of letters groups one kind of requirement: FCL for licences, MED for medical, ORO for the operator's organisation, CAT for commercial transport, SPA for specific approvals (RVSM, low visibility, dangerous goods…). Knowing the letters helps you find the rule.
</Callout>
