## The treaty that started it all

The <Term id="chicago-convention" /> on International Civil Aviation was signed in 1944, at the end of the Second World War, so that civil aviation could develop **safely and in an orderly way**. Its main ideas:

- **Sovereignty**: every State has **complete and exclusive** sovereignty over the airspace above its territory (Article 1).
- **Nationality**: an aircraft has the nationality of the State in which it is **registered**, and it cannot be registered in two States at once.
- **Common rules**: States undertake to work together to achieve the greatest possible uniformity in regulations and procedures.

The Convention created <Term id="icao" /> (the International Civil Aviation Organization), which began work in 1947 and is today a United Nations agency based in Montreal.

## The Annexes

ICAO adopts its rules in the **Annexes** to the Convention, organised by subject: licensing (Annex 1), rules of the air (2), meteorology (3), operation of aircraft (6), air traffic services (11), aerodromes (14), accident investigation (13), security (17), dangerous goods (18), among others.

They contain two kinds of provisions:

| Type | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Standard** | Its uniform application is considered **necessary**; States must comply |
| **Recommended practice** | Its application is considered **desirable** |

A State that cannot comply with a standard must **notify the difference** to ICAO (Article 38), and differences are published in its AIP so that others know about them.

## The freedoms of the air

International commercial traffic is based on rights that States grant each other, known as the <Term id="freedoms-of-the-air" />:

| Freedom | Right | Example with a Spanish airline |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | Fly over another State without landing | Madrid–Warsaw over France |
| **2nd** | Land in another State for non-traffic purposes (fuel, maintenance) | Technical stop in Iceland |
| **3rd** | Carry passengers from its own country to another | Madrid → Bogotá |
| **4th** | Bring passengers from another country to its own | Bogotá → Madrid |
| **5th** | Carry passengers between two foreign countries, as part of a flight that starts or ends in its own country | Madrid → Bogotá → Lima, selling the Bogotá–Lima leg |

The first two are in a multilateral agreement from 1944 (the Transit Agreement). The others are negotiated in **bilateral agreements** between States or, in the EU, in the single aviation market. **Cabotage** (flights within another country) is heavily restricted.

## Registration and documents

Every aircraft carries **nationality and registration marks**; Spanish ones begin with **EC-**. Article 29 of the Convention lists the documents every aircraft must carry on an international flight:

- the **certificate of registration**;
- the **certificate of airworthiness**;
- the **licences** of each crew member;
- the **journey log book**;
- the **radio station licence**, if it has a radio;
- the **passenger list** and **cargo manifest**, if it carries them.
