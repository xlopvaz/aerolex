## The components

The <Term id="ils" /> guides the aircraft to the runway in both planes:

- The <Term id="localizer" /> (LOC) gives **lateral** guidance. It transmits on VHF, between **108.10 and 111.95 MHz**, from an antenna at the **far end** of the runway.
- The <Term id="glide-path">glide slope</Term> (GS or GP) gives **vertical** guidance. It transmits on UHF from an antenna beside the runway, about 750–1,250 ft from the threshold. The path is usually **3°**.
- **Marker beacons** (or a DME) give distance: the outer marker is where the glide slope is intercepted, about 1,400 ft above the runway, and the middle marker where the path is at about 200 ft.

## Far more sensitive than a VOR

The localizer beam is adjusted to be about **700 ft wide at the runway threshold** from side to side, which usually gives a total angle of about **5°**: full-scale deflection corresponds to about **2.5°** each side. The glide slope is about **1.4°** thick, that is, about **0.7°** above and below. Compared with a VOR's 10°, the ILS is **about four times more sensitive**: corrections must be small and early.

## False glide paths

The glide slope antenna also produces **false paths at higher angles** (the lowest, according to the FAA Instrument Flying Handbook, at about 9°–12°). That is why the glide slope is intercepted **from below**, at the published altitude, and the height is checked when passing the final approach fix.

## Decision altitude

On a precision approach, at the **decision altitude (or height)** (DA/DH) the pilot must have the **visual references** needed to continue. Otherwise, they start the <Term id="missed-approach" />. In CAT I the DA is read on the barometric altimeter; in CAT II and III the DH is measured with the radio altimeter.

## Types and categories of operation

ICAO rules classify instrument approaches as:

- **Type A**: a minimum descent height or decision height of **75 m (250 ft) or more**.
- **Type B**: a decision height **below 75 m (250 ft)**, divided into categories:

| Category | Decision height | Visibility or <Term id="rvr" /> |
|---|---|---|
| **CAT I** | Not lower than **60 m (200 ft)** | Visibility ≥ 800 m or RVR ≥ **550 m** |
| **CAT II** | Lower than 60 m but not lower than **30 m (100 ft)** | RVR ≥ **300 m** |
| **CAT IIIA** | Lower than 30 m or no DH | RVR ≥ **175 m** |
| **CAT IIIB** | Lower than 15 m (50 ft) or no DH | RVR below 175 m and ≥ **50 m** |
| **CAT IIIC** | No DH | No RVR limit |

CAT II and III also require operator approval, trained crews, certified aircraft and airports with low-visibility procedures.
