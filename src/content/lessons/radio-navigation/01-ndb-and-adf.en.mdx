## The simplest radio aid

An <Term id="ndb" /> transmits in all directions on **long and medium wave** (in the USA, normally between 190 and 535 kHz). In the aircraft, the <Term id="adf" /> has a needle that **points to the station**. There are no radials and no TO/FROM indication: only the direction to the beacon.

Like any radio aid, it is **identified** by listening to its Morse code before use.

## Relative bearing

**Relative bearing** (RB) is the angle between the aircraft's **nose** and the station, measured clockwise. A fixed-card ADF shows it directly: if the needle reads 090, the station is to the aircraft's right, 90° from the nose.

To know where the station is relative to magnetic north:

> magnetic bearing to the station (QDM) = magnetic heading + relative bearing

(subtracting 360 if the result is over 360).

Example: magnetic heading **300°**, relative bearing **090°** → 300 + 90 = 390 − 360 = **030°**. To fly to the station with no wind you would steer 030°.

## QDM and QDR

These are ICAO Q codes still in use:

- **QDM**: the magnetic heading to steer (with no wind) to reach **the** station.
- **QDR**: the aircraft's magnetic bearing **from** the station.

They always differ by **180°**: if the QDM is 030°, the QDR is 210°.

## The RMI

The **radio magnetic indicator** (RMI) combines a card that turns with the aircraft's heading and one or two needles for the ADF or VOR. That way the needle's **head** reads the **QDM** directly and its **tail** the **QDR**, with no calculation.

## ADF errors

Long and medium waves have limitations the pilot needs to know:

| Error | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Thunderstorms** | The needle may point to lightning instead of the station |
| **Night effect** | At night, waves reflected off the ionosphere interfere with the direct wave and the needle swings |
| **Coastal refraction** | The wave changes direction as it crosses the coastline |
| **Mountain effect** | Mountains reflect the signal and deflect the indication |

Also, not all equipment gives a reliable failure warning: if you cannot hear the identifier, the indication is not reliable.

<Callout type="tip" label="Following a needle is not following a track">
If you always fly with the needle on the nose (*homing*), with a crosswind you reach the station along a curve. To follow a straight track you apply a wind correction and keep the needle offset to the opposite side.
</Callout>
