## Radials

A <Term id="vor" /> transmits on **VHF**, between **108.0 and 117.95 MHz**, a signal that lets the receiver work out which **radial** it is on. A <Term id="radial" /> is a **magnetic** bearing **from** the station: the 090 radial is the line of positions magnetic east of the VOR.

Being VHF, the signal travels in a **straight line**: range increases with altitude and mountains block it.

## The CDI and the TO/FROM indication

A course is selected with the OBS. The indicator shows:

- the CDI **bar**: how far off the course you are. Full-scale deflection is about **10°**, each dot about **2°**;
- the **TO/FROM** indicator: whether the selected course takes you **towards** the station or **away** from it.

With the OBS on 090 and a **FROM** indication, you are on the 090 radial. With 090 and **TO**, you are on the 270 radial, flying towards the station.

## Reverse sensing

The bar shows the correct way to correct only if the aircraft's **heading** is close to the **selected course**. If you fly the opposite way (for example, course 090 selected and heading 270), the bar seems to show the opposite of what you should do: this is **reverse sensing**. The answer is always to select a course close to the heading being flown.

An HSI, which turns the card with the heading, avoids this problem.

## Over the station

Right above the VOR there is a **cone of confusion**: the bar swings and TO changes to FROM. It is the sign that you have passed over the station.

## Checking the receiver

According to the FAA AIM, the VOR receiver is checked with these tolerances:

| Check | Tolerance |
|---|---|
| Test station (VOT) or ground checkpoint | **± 4°** |
| Airborne checkpoint | **± 6°** |
| Two receivers tuned to the same VOR | **4°** maximum difference |

When a VOR is under maintenance its Morse **identification is removed**: a station with no identifier must not be used.

## DME

<Term id="dme" /> measures **distance** to the ground equipment by timing how long an interrogation from the aircraft takes to get a reply. It works on **UHF** and is usually paired with a VOR or an ILS.

It measures **slant range** (a straight line to the antenna), not distance over the ground. Over the station, an aircraft at **6,000 ft** reads about **1 NM** even though it is directly overhead. The AIM says the error is small if the aircraft is at least **1 NM from the station for every 1,000 ft** of height above it.

<Callout type="info" label="Orientation example">
OBS on 330, bar deflected left, TO indication, heading 330. The course to the station is to your left: correct to the left. You are east of the 150 radial, which is the one that leads to the station on a heading of 330.
</Callout>
