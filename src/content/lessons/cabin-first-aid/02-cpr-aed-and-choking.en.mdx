## Recognising cardiac arrest

According to the 2021 European Resuscitation Council (ERC) guidelines, suspect **cardiac arrest** in a person who:

- is **unresponsive**, and
- is **not breathing normally**.

**Agonal breathing** (slow, noisy, irregular gasps) is **not** normal breathing: it is a sign of arrest. If in doubt, act as if it were one.

## The sequence

1. **Call for help** and get someone to bring the <Term id="aed" />; inform the flight deck.
2. Start **chest compressions**:
   - in the **centre of the chest** (lower half of the breastbone);
   - to a depth of **at least 5 cm but not more than 6 cm**;
   - at a rate of **100 to 120 per minute**;
   - letting the chest **recoil fully** after each compression;
   - with **minimal interruptions**.
3. If trained and willing, alternate **30 compressions and 2 rescue breaths**.
4. **Attach the AED** as soon as it arrives and follow its prompts.
5. Do not stop until the person **shows signs of life**, help arrives to take over or the rescuers are exhausted.

<Term id="cpr" /> is exhausting: if there are several rescuers, **take turns** every few minutes to keep the quality up.

## On board

- Compressions must be given on a **firm surface**: on an aircraft, usually on the **floor**, in the aisle or the galley.
- Space is tight: organise the crew and, if needed, move other passengers.
- While the AED is in use, **nobody may touch** the patient during analysis or the shock.

## Choking

If an adult is choking:

1. If they **can cough**, encourage them to keep coughing.
2. If coughing stops being effective: up to **5 back blows**, between the shoulder blades, with the person leaning forward.
3. If that does not work: up to **5 abdominal thrusts**.
4. **Alternate** back blows and thrusts while they remain conscious.
5. If they become unresponsive: start **CPR**.

## The recovery position

A person who is **unresponsive but breathing normally** is placed on their side, in the **recovery position**, to keep the airway open. Their breathing is monitored continuously, because they may deteriorate.
