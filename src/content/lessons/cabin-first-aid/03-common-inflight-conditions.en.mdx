## Fainting

It is the most frequent problem on board. The person feels dizzy, goes pale, sweats and may lose consciousness for a few seconds. Heat, dehydration, not eating, alcohol and long periods sitting or standing all make it more likely.

- **Lay** the person down if possible, or recline them, and loosen tight clothing.
- If they come round quickly, give them **water** and keep an eye on them.
- If they are **unresponsive**, apply what you saw in the previous lesson: check whether they are breathing normally.

## Hypoglycaemia

People with diabetes can suffer **low blood sugar**: sudden behavioural changes, confusion, sweating, shaking, pallor. The ERC guidelines say:

- if the person is **conscious and able to swallow**, give **15 to 20 g of glucose** as tablets or, if there are none, another sugar (juice, a sugary soft drink);
- if there is no improvement in about **15 minutes**, repeat;
- if they cannot swallow or lose consciousness, give **nothing** by mouth and seek medical help.

## Anaphylaxis

A **severe allergic reaction** (for example, to nuts) can compromise breathing and circulation: swollen lips and tongue, difficulty breathing, hoarseness, dizziness, rash.

- The first-line treatment is **intramuscular adrenaline**. Many people with allergies carry their own **auto-injector**: **help them use it** on the outer thigh.
- If they do not improve within about **5 minutes**, a **second dose** may be given.
- Inform the commander: anaphylaxis may require a diversion.

## Asthma

In an asthma attack, help the person use **their own** bronchodilator **inhaler**, keep them sitting upright and reassure them. If they do not improve, they may need oxygen and medical help.

## Stroke

A **stroke** needs hospital treatment as soon as possible. The ERC recommends using a simple scale to recognise it, such as **FAST**:

| Letter | What to check |
|---|---|
| **F** (face) | Does the mouth droop when they smile? |
| **A** (arms) | Can they raise both arms and keep them up? |
| **S** (speech) | Is their speech slurred or hard to understand? |
| **T** (time) | Note the time it started and **report it at once** |

The time the symptoms began is important information for doctors.

## Burns

Burns from hot liquids are a real risk in the cabin.

- **Cool** the burn with cool water for **at least 20 minutes**, starting as soon as possible.
- Remove rings and clothing that is not stuck to the skin.
- Then cover with a clean, non-stick dressing, or cling film.
- Do not apply home remedies or ice directly.
