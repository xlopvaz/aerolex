## A common language to describe a flight

So that safety reports from all over the world can be compared, ICAO and CAST (the US Commercial Aviation Safety Team) set up a joint team, **CICTT**, which defined the **phases of flight** with precise limits. Those are the ones used in this lesson.

## The phases, in order

  ["Standing", "The aircraft is stationary at the stand (boarding, loading, preparation)"],
  ["Pushback/towing", "A tug pushes or tows the aircraft"],
  ["Taxi", "The aircraft moves under its own power along the taxiways"],
  ["Take-off", "From the application of take-off power to 35 ft above the runway"],
  ["Initial climb", "To the first prescribed power reduction or 1,000 ft above the runway, whichever comes first"],
  ["En route", "Climb to cruise, cruise, level changes, descent and holding"],
  ["Approach", "From the start of the approach until the landing flare begins"],
  ["Landing", "From the flare until the aircraft leaves the runway or stops"],
  ["Taxi", "From the runway to the stand"]

A few notes:

- The <Term id="pushback" /> exists because most jets cannot (or should not) reverse out of the stand on their own.
- **Taxiing** is done along the <Term id="taxiway">taxiways</Term> to the holding point of the <Term id="runway" />.
- The **en route** phase includes <Term id="holding" />, when ATC asks the aircraft to fly a racetrack pattern before continuing.
- The **approach** includes the **missed approach**: if the landing is not safe, the crew flies a <Term id="go-around" /> and climbs away.
- The **flare** is the final manoeuvre in which the pilot raises the nose slightly just before touching down.

The taxonomy adds phases for non-routine situations (emergency descent, uncontrolled descent, post-impact, unknown) and one for low-level **manoeuvring**, such as aerobatics or crop-spraying.

## The critical phases

Regulation (EU) No 965/2012 defines the **critical phases of flight** for aeroplanes as the take-off run, the take-off flight path, the final approach, the missed approach, the landing (including the landing roll) and any other phase the pilot-in-command decides. They are the moments of highest workload, which is why the <Term id="sterile-flight-deck" /> rule applies: only flight-related conversation is allowed.

<Callout type="tip" label="Make it stick">
Next time you fly, notice the cues: the pushback (the aircraft moves without engine noise), the taxi, the take-off, the chime when the seat-belt sign goes off… Each one marks a change of phase.
</Callout>
