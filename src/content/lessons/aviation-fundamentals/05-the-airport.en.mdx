## Aerodrome and airport

Regulations talk about an **aerodrome**: any area of land or water prepared for the arrival, departure and surface movement of aircraft. An **airport** is, in practice, an aerodrome with facilities and services for passenger or cargo transport.

## Where an aircraft moves

| Area | What it includes | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **Manoeuvring area** | <Term id="runway">Runways</Term> and <Term id="taxiway">taxiways</Term> | Taking off, landing and taxiing |
| <Term id="apron">Apron</Term> | Aircraft stands and their access lanes | Boarding, loading, refuelling and turnaround maintenance |
| **Movement area** | The manoeuvring area plus the aprons | Everywhere an aircraft goes on the ground |

Movement on the manoeuvring area is cleared by the **control tower** (<Term id="atc" />). At many large airports the apron has its own management service.

## How runways are numbered

A runway's number is its **magnetic heading divided by 10 and rounded**, as seen from the direction of approach:

- A runway aligned on 143° magnetic is runway **14**. From the other end it is seen on 323°, so that end is runway **32**. The two numbers of a runway always differ by 18.
- Parallel runways get a letter: **L** (left), **C** (centre) and **R** (right), as seen from the approach. That is why 14L is 32R from the opposite end.
- Under the ICAO rule, single-digit numbers have a leading zero: a runway aligned on 087° is runway **09**.

At the start of each runway is the <Term id="threshold" />, marked with white stripes, with the runway number painted next to it.

## Colours worth knowing

- **Surface markings:** runway markings are **white**; taxiway and apron markings are **yellow**.
- **Mandatory instruction signs:** **white letters on a red background**. They mark, for example, the entrance to a runway: do not cross without a clearance.
- **Location signs:** **yellow letters on a black background**. They tell you which taxiway you are on.
- **Direction signs:** **black letters on a yellow background**, with an arrow towards the taxiway shown.
