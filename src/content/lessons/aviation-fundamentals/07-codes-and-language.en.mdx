## Two codes for every airport

| | <Term id="iata" /> code | <Term id="icao" /> code |
|---|---|---|
| Letters | 3 | 4 |
| Used by | Passengers, tickets, baggage, reservations | Pilots, air traffic control, flight plans, METAR and TAF |
| Madrid-Barajas | MAD | LEMD |
| Barcelona-El Prat | BCN | LEBL |
| Gran Canaria | LPA | GCLP |
| New York JFK | JFK | KJFK |

IATA codes follow no geographical rule. ICAO codes (Doc 7910) do: the **first letter** shows a large region and the first two letters usually identify the country.

- **L**: southern Europe → **LE** mainland Spain and the Balearic Islands, **LF** France, **LP** Portugal, **LI** Italy.
- **E**: northern Europe → **EG** United Kingdom, **ED** Germany.
- **G**: western Africa, including the **Canary Islands** (**GC**).
- **K**: the contiguous United States, usually a "K" in front of the three-letter code (KJFK).

## Airlines: two letters, three letters and a call sign

| Airline | IATA | ICAO | Radiotelephony call sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iberia | IB | IBE | IBERIA |
| Vueling | VY | VLG | VUELING |
| Air Europa | UX | AEA | EUROPA |
| Ryanair | FR | RYR | RYANAIR |

## The aircraft: type and registration

- **Type designator** (Doc 8643): a code of up to four characters for each model, used in flight plans. For example, **A20N** (A320neo), **B738** (737-800), **A359** (A350-900) or **B789** (787-9).
- **Registration** (Annex 7): like a car's number plate, unique to each aircraft. It starts with the **nationality mark**: **EC** Spain, **F** France, **G** United Kingdom, **D** Germany, **N** United States.

## Spelling on the radio: the phonetic alphabet

On the radio, letters are replaced by the words of the ICAO <Term id="phonetic-alphabet" /> (Annex 10) so they cannot be confused:

**A** Alfa · **B** Bravo · **C** Charlie · **D** Delta · **E** Echo · **F** Foxtrot · **G** Golf · **H** Hotel · **I** India · **J** Juliett · **K** Kilo · **L** Lima · **M** Mike · **N** November · **O** Oscar · **P** Papa · **Q** Quebec · **R** Romeo · **S** Sierra · **T** Tango · **U** Uniform · **V** Victor · **W** Whiskey · **X** X-ray · **Y** Yankee · **Z** Zulu

So a Spanish registration such as EC-ABC is read "Echo Charlie Alfa Bravo Charlie". Some numbers also have special pronunciations to avoid confusion, such as **"tree"** (3), **"fife"** (5) and **"niner"** (9).

## Time: always UTC

Aviation uses <Term id="utc" />, also called **Zulu** time (that is why METAR and TAF times end in "Z"), and the 24-hour clock. That removes any doubt between countries or around daylight-saving changes.

- Mainland Spain and the Balearic Islands: **UTC+1** in winter and **UTC+2** in summer.
- Canary Islands: **UTC+0** in winter and **UTC+1** in summer.

Example: a departure at **1430Z** in July is at 16:30 in Madrid and 15:30 in Las Palmas.
