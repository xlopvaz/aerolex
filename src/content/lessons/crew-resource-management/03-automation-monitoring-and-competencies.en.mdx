## Pilot flying and pilot monitoring

In a two-pilot crew, tasks are shared:

| <Term id="pf" /> (pilot flying) | <Term id="pm" /> (pilot monitoring) |
|---|---|
| Controls the flight path, by hand or through the automation | **Monitors** the flight path and systems |
| Decides mode changes and announces them | Checks and confirms |
| | Radio, checklists, configuration requested by the PF |

Who is PF does not depend on rank: captain and first officer alternate, but the **captain** remains responsible and can take control.

## Monitoring is an active task

Many events happen with a pilot who "looked" without seeing. Monitoring well means:

- knowing **what should happen** (path, speed, modes) and checking that it does;
- **calling out** deviations: "Speed", "Sink rate", "Glide slope";
- sharing attention and protecting it from distractions in critical phases.

## Automation

The autopilot and autothrust reduce workload, but you must **know what they are doing**:

- Every mode change is read on the <Term id="fma" /> and **called out**: the PF announces it and the PM confirms it.
- Use the **appropriate level of automation** for the situation. If the automation does not do what is expected and you don't understand why, step down a level: simpler modes or manual flying.
- The key question: **"What is it doing? Why? What will it do next?"**

## The sterile flight deck

In critical phases (taxi, take-off, climb and approach, in practice below **10,000 ft** except in cruise) the <Term id="sterile-flight-deck" /> applies: only operationally necessary conversation. The cabin crew calls the flight deck only for safety reasons.

## The nine competencies

Modern pilot training, including <Term id="ebt" />, is organised around the nine competencies defined by ICAO:

| Code | Competency |
|---|---|
| **KNO** | Application of knowledge |
| **PRO** | Application of procedures and compliance with regulations |
| **COM** | Communication |
| **FPA** | Flight path management: automation |
| **FPM** | Flight path management: manual control |
| **LTW** | Leadership and teamwork |
| **PSD** | Problem solving and decision making |
| **SAW** | Situation awareness and management of information |
| **WLM** | Workload management |

Seven of the nine are not about "handling the aircraft": they show how much CRM is part of the job.

<Callout type="info" label="From CRM to competencies">
CRM began as a separate course. Today it is assessed within every simulator session, by observing specific behaviours for each competency.
</Callout>
