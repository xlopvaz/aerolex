## A tool without electricity

The <Term id="magnetic-compass">magnetic compass</Term> is a magnetised card floating in liquid that lines up with the Earth's magnetic field. It needs neither electricity nor air: that is why it remains in every cockpit as a standby instrument. In exchange, it has errors you need to know.

## Variation

Magnetic north is not the same as true north. <Term id="magnetic-variation">Variation</Term> is the angle between them and **depends on location**:

- lines joining points of equal variation are called **isogonic lines**;
- the line of zero variation is the **agonic line**.

To go from **true** to **magnetic** heading: **east is subtracted** and **west is added** ("east is least, west is best").

Example: true heading 090°, variation 3° E → magnetic heading **087°**.

## Deviation

The aircraft's own metal and electrical equipment deflect the compass. That error is **deviation**, and unlike variation it **depends on heading**, not location. It is measured and reduced in a process called **swinging the compass**, and what remains is written on a **deviation card** next to the instrument.

The complete chain is: **true → (variation) → magnetic → (deviation) → compass**.

## Magnetic dip

The magnetic field lines are not horizontal: they dip towards the ground, more and more steeply nearer the poles. That **dip** pulls the card downwards and causes acceleration and turning errors. What follows applies to the **northern hemisphere**; in the south the effects are reversed.

## Acceleration error: ANDS

On **east or west** headings:

- when you **Accelerate**, the compass shows a turn towards **North**;
- when you **Decelerate**, it shows a turn towards **South**.

The error goes away once speed is steady. On north or south headings it is minimal.

## Turning error

It is greatest when turning **from** or **towards** north or south:

- **Towards north**, the compass **lags**: roll out **before** it shows the desired heading.
- **Towards south**, the compass **leads**: roll out **after** it.

The PHAK gives the approximate amount as **15° plus half the latitude**. Example at 40° N: 15 + 20 = **35°**. To end up heading north, start rolling the wings level when the compass reads about 035° (turning right from west) or 325° (turning left from east).

## Oscillation

In turbulence the card swings. To set the heading indicator, use the **average** of the swings.

<Callout type="tip" label="To remember">
ANDS: accelerate → north, decelerate → south. And for turns: towards north roll out early, towards south roll out late.
</Callout>
