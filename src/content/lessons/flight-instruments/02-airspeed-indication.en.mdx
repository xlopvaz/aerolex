## The airspeed indicator's colours

On light aircraft, the airspeed indicator has standard markings:

| Marking | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **White arc** | Flap operating range: from V<sub>S0</sub> (stall in landing configuration) to V<sub>FE</sub> (maximum flaps extended) |
| **Green arc** | Normal operating range: from V<sub>S1</sub> (stall in a given configuration, usually clean) to V<sub>NO</sub> (maximum structural cruising speed) |
| **Yellow arc** | Caution: smooth air only |
| **Red line** | V<sub>NE</sub>, never-exceed speed |
| **Blue line** (twins) | V<sub>YSE</sub>, best single-engine rate of climb |

Other important speeds are **not** shown on the instrument and come from placards or the manual: manoeuvring speed (V<sub>A</sub>), gear speeds (V<sub>LO</sub>, V<sub>LE</sub>), best angle and best rate of climb (V<sub>X</sub>, V<sub>Y</sub>) and the minimum control speed with one engine (V<sub>MC</sub>).

## From indicated to true

1. **<Term id="ias">IAS</Term>**: the direct instrument reading.
2. **<Term id="cas">CAS</Term>**: IAS corrected for instrument and installation errors. The error is greatest at low speed, when the angle of attack is high and the ports "see" the air at an angle.
3. **EAS**: CAS corrected for air **compressibility**, which only matters at high speed and high altitude.
4. **<Term id="tas">TAS</Term>**: the real speed through the air, corrected for altitude and temperature (that is, for density). A PHAK rule of thumb: TAS exceeds CAS by **2 % per 1,000 ft** of altitude.
5. **<Term id="ground-speed">Groundspeed</Term>**: TAS plus or minus the effect of the wind. It falls with a headwind and rises with a tailwind.

Example with the 2 % rule: at 10,000 ft with a CAS of 150 kt, the TAS is about 150 × 1.20 = **180 kt**. With a 30 kt headwind, the groundspeed is about **150 kt**.

## Why each matters

- **Aerodynamic performance** (stall, flap limits) depends on dynamic pressure: it is flown in **IAS/CAS**. That is why the indicated stall speed is almost the same at any altitude.
- **Navigation** (times, fuel) depends on **TAS** and groundspeed.

## VMO and MMO

Jets have two speed limits:

- **V<sub>MO</sub>**: maximum operating speed, in IAS/CAS. It limits at low and medium altitude, where the issue is **structural load**.
- **M<sub>MO</sub>**: maximum operating <Term id="mach-number">Mach number</Term>. It limits at high altitude, where the issue is **compressibility**: shock waves, buffet and effects on control.

Because Mach number increases with altitude at constant IAS, in a climb the limit changes from V<sub>MO</sub> to M<sub>MO</sub>. On modern displays the limit appears as a **red and black band** (*barber pole*) on the speed tape, which moves depending on which of the two is limiting.

<Callout type="tip" label="What the tape tells you">
On a PFD, the speed tape also shows an amber or red band at the bottom, the minimum or stall-protection speed, which changes with weight, configuration and load factor.
</Callout>
