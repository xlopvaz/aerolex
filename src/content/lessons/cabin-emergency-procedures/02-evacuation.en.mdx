## Who decides

The decision to evacuate normally belongs to the <Term id="pilot-in-command">commander</Term>, who has the overall picture: the state of the aircraft, the engines, the outside and the emergency services.

But the cabin crew may face an obvious danger that the flight deck cannot see. SKYbrary sets out the usual criteria for a cabin crew member to **start the evacuation on their own initiative**:

- **fire** or **smoke** that cannot be controlled;
- severe **structural damage**;
- a **ditching**;
- an **abnormal aircraft attitude**;
- and, in every case, **no response** from the flight deck.

First, the crew member must **try to contact** the pilots. And they may only start it with the aircraft **stopped** and the engines shut down. If one crew member starts an evacuation, all the others follow the procedure at once.

## The 90 seconds

To be certificated, a transport aircraft must show that it can be fully evacuated in **90 seconds** using only half of its exits. It is a design test, not a promise: in a real accident there is smoke, darkness, injured people and unusable exits. But it shows how fast you have to act.

## Assessing the exit before opening it

Before opening an <Term id="emergency-exit">exit</Term>, the crew member looks through the window and checks that outside there is **no**:

- **fire** or smoke;
- **water** above the door sill;
- **obstruction** or debris;
- **height** or slope that would make the slide dangerous.

If the exit cannot be used, it is **blocked** with the body and passengers are sent to another one with clear commands.

When the door is opened with the slide armed, the slide inflates by itself. If it does not, the **manual inflation handle** is pulled. The crew member protects themselves next to the door, holding the assist handle, so as not to be pulled out.

## The commands

Commands must be **short, very loud and repeated**. A typical sequence:

> "Release your seat belts! Get out! Leave everything!"
>
> *«¡Suelten los cinturones! ¡Salgan! ¡Dejen todo!»*

And at the door: "Come this way! Jump! Move away!" / *«¡Vengan por aquí! ¡Salten! ¡Salgan del avión!»*

Accident studies show that many passengers try to take their **cabin baggage**, which slows the flow, blocks aisles and exits and can damage the slide. That is why the command to leave everything matters so much, and why the passenger briefing beforehand should include it.

## Outside the aircraft

- Passengers must **move away** from the aircraft and not stay at the foot of the slide.
- Chosen <Term id="able-bodied-passenger">able-bodied passengers</Term> can help others at the foot of the slide.
- Cabin crew leave when nobody is left in their area, checking if it is safe to do so, and then **gather** the passengers, count who is there and look after the injured until the emergency services arrive.
