## The clock is running

An on-board fire is one of the most serious emergencies, because there is nowhere to go and the aircraft takes time to land. FAA Advisory Circular AC 120-80B stresses two ideas: act **immediately and decisively**, and **communicate** without delay with the flight deck, which must prepare to land while the cabin fights the fire.

## Working as a team

Procedures share the work among several crew members:

- one **fights** the fire with the extinguisher;
- one **communicates** with the flight deck and reports the situation and how it develops;
- one **brings** more extinguishers and protective equipment and helps move passengers.

Meanwhile, passengers are **moved away** from the area if possible.

## Extinguishers

| Agent | For | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Halon** | Fires of any kind, including electrical ones | It stops the chemical reaction; in an enclosed space, respiratory protection is advisable |
| **Water** | Solid materials: paper, fabric, seats | **Not** on live electrical equipment or on grease |

## The smoke hood

<Term id="pbe">Protective breathing equipment</Term> (the smoke hood) protects the head and gives oxygen to the crew member fighting the fire. According to EASA, it must protect for **at least 15 minutes** at normal cabin altitude. Putting it on before approaching the smoke keeps the crew member from being incapacitated.

## Hidden fires

Smoke or a burning smell with no visible flames may indicate a fire **behind a panel** or in the wiring. AC 120-80B says to look for the source, for example by feeling panels with the **back of the hand** for heat, and to create an opening to apply the extinguishing agent. A hidden fire must not be considered dealt with just because the smoke can no longer be seen.

## Galleys and lavatories

- **Oven**: switch off the power and keep the **door closed** to smother the fire.
- **Lavatory**: every waste bin has an automatic extinguisher and there is a smoke detector; if it goes off, the lavatory is checked carefully, feeling the door first.

## Lithium batteries

Personal devices (phones, laptops, power banks) can go into <Term id="thermal-runaway" />: the battery heats itself, gives off smoke and can catch fire and spread from cell to cell. FAA SAFO 09013 proposes a **two-phase** procedure:

1. **Put out the fire** with the extinguisher (halon or water).
2. **Cool** the device by soaking it in **water or other non-alcoholic liquids**, so that the other cells do not go into runaway.

Also:

- **Do not move** a device that is burning, smoking or very hot: the FAA maintains this recommendation even where containment bags are available.
- If the device is plugged in, **disconnect** it from the aircraft socket.
- ICAO checklists for these cases also call for **informing the commander** and **moving passengers away**, and then monitoring the device for the rest of the flight.
