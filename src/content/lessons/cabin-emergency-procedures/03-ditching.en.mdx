This lesson summarises general principles. The exact commands, positions, checklists and division of tasks are set by each airline's operations manual and approved training.

## What changes on water

A <Term id="ditching" /> is a planned emergency landing on water. It has its own features:

- Expect **more than one impact**: the aircraft may bounce or touch the water tail first.
- Some exits may end up **below the waterline** and must not be opened.
- Cold water very quickly reduces the ability to swim and hold on.

## Life jackets

The <Term id="life-jacket">life jacket</Term> is put on in the seat, but **inflated when leaving the aircraft**, never inside. A life jacket inflated inside the cabin:

- makes it hard to move along the aisle and through the exits;
- can trap a person if the cabin floods, because it pushes them up against the ceiling.

Small children wear special life jackets, often attached to the accompanying adult's. Information on life jackets is part of the safety briefing on flights over water.

<Callout type="caution" label="The key instruction">
"Put on your life jacket, but do not inflate it until you leave the aircraft." / *«Pónganse el chaleco, pero no lo inflen hasta salir del avión.»*
</Callout>

## Rafts and slide-rafts

Depending on the aircraft and the route, evacuation onto water uses:

- **slide-rafts**: slides that, once in the water, are detached from the aircraft and used as rafts;
- separate **rafts** that are taken out, launched and inflated at the exits;
- on some short-haul aircraft, only life jackets, flotation seat cushions and the wings themselves as a platform.

EU rules require life jackets for all occupants on flights over water beyond a certain distance from land, and rafts and survival equipment on long flights over water. The operator's manual details what each aircraft carries.

## In the water

The priorities, in roughly this order, are:

1. **Move** the raft away from the aircraft, cutting the mooring line if necessary.
2. **Pick up** people in the water.
3. **Protect** against cold and water: hypothermia is the great enemy.
4. **Signal** the position with the survival equipment: lights, mirror, flares, locator beacon.
5. **Keep up morale** and organise lookout shifts.

<Callout type="info" label="Dialogue · Diálogo">
**Cabin crew:** Life jackets on! Do not inflate them inside! Come towards me!

**Passenger:** Shall I inflate it now?

**Cabin crew:** No, as you go out of the door. Keep moving!

*TCP: ¡Chalecos puestos! ¡No los inflen dentro! ¡Vengan hacia mí! — Pasajero: ¿Lo inflo ya? — TCP: No, al salir por la puerta. ¡Siga avanzando!*
</Callout>
