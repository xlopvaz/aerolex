## The accident categories

| Category | What it is | Main defence |
|---|---|---|
| <Term id="loc-i" /> | Loss of control in flight | Upset prevention and recovery (Lesson 3) |
| <Term id="cfit" /> | Hitting terrain with a controllable aircraft | Height awareness, charts, EGPWS |
| <Term id="runway-excursion" /> | Going off the side or the end | Stabilised approach, performance calculation |
| <Term id="runway-incursion" /> | Incorrect presence on an active runway | Phraseology, readback, stop bars |
| **Mid-air collision** | Two aircraft at the same point | ATC, lookout, TCAS |

## CFIT and EGPWS

CFIT almost always comes from losing **position or height awareness**: a wrong QNH, an early descent, a misread chart. The <Term id="egpws" /> warns with several modes:

| Mode | Warns of… | Typical alert |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Excessive descent rate | "SINK RATE", "PULL UP" |
| **2** | Excessive closure with terrain | "TERRAIN TERRAIN", "PULL UP" |
| **3** | Height loss after take-off | "DON'T SINK" |
| **4** | Low height without landing configuration | "TOO LOW GEAR", "TOO LOW FLAPS", "TOO LOW TERRAIN" |
| **5** | Below the glide slope | "GLIDESLOPE" |
| **6** | Altitude callouts and excessive bank | "BANK ANGLE", height callouts |

The enhanced version adds a **look-ahead** function with a terrain database: it warns earlier, with "TERRAIN AHEAD".

With a **PULL UP**, the response is **immediate** and unquestioned: maximum thrust, wings level, pitch up to the limit given in the procedure and speed brakes retracted; held until the warning stops.

## The stabilised approach

Many runway excursions start with an **unstable** approach. The Flight Safety Foundation recommends that the aircraft be stabilised by **1,000 ft** above the aerodrome in IMC and **500 ft** in VMC. It is <Term id="stabilised-approach">stabilised</Term> if:

- it is on the **correct path** and needs only small heading and pitch changes;
- speed is no more than **VREF + 20 kt** and no less than **VREF**;
- it is in **landing configuration**;
- descent rate is no more than **1,000 ft/min**;
- **thrust** is appropriate;
- **briefings and checklists** are complete.

If the approach becomes unstabilised below that height: **go around**, no exceptions. Many operators use a single height for all conditions, which makes it easier to track with FDM.

## Mid-air collision and TCAS

<Term id="tcas" /> first gives a **traffic advisory (TA)** and, if the risk grows, a **resolution advisory (RA)** with a vertical command. An RA is followed **always and immediately**, even against ATC, and reported (Module 14). Both aircraft coordinate their RAs so they manoeuvre in opposite directions: if one does not follow it, the protection is lost.

<Callout type="caution" label="Pressure to land">
The most common reason for continuing an unstable approach is pressure to get in: schedule, fatigue, "almost there". The go-around is a normal manoeuvre, not a failure.
</Callout>
