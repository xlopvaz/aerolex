## What is an upset?

An <Term id="upset" /> is an aircraft unintentionally exceeding the parameters normal in line operations:

- pitch more than **25° nose up**;
- pitch more than **10° nose down**;
- bank more than **45°**;
- or, within those limits, an **airspeed inappropriate** for the conditions.

Causes can be **environmental** (turbulence, <Term id="wake-turbulence" />, icing, wind shear), **systems** (autopilot failures, erroneous indications) or **crew** (distraction, automation confusion, poor energy management). <Term id="loc-i" /> is one of the leading causes of fatal accidents in commercial aviation.

## Surprise

An unexpected situation causes **startle**: for a few seconds it is hard to think, and the instinctive reaction (pulling back, for example) can make everything worse. That is why UPRT training aims to make recovery actions **automatic**, and the golden rule is: **recognise, confirm and recover**.

## Stall recovery

In a <Term id="stall" />, the wing exceeds its critical <Term id="angle-of-attack" />. Recovery always starts by **reducing the angle of attack**, even if height is lost:

1. Disconnect the **autopilot** and **autothrust**.
2. **Lower the nose** until the stall warnings stop.
3. **Level the wings**.
4. Adjust **thrust** as needed (on aircraft with engines under the wings, a sharp thrust increase pitches the nose up).
5. Return to the desired **flight path** without causing a secondary stall.

<Callout type="warning" label="You don't get out of a stall by pulling">
Height and speed do not matter if the wing is stalled. While the angle of attack is excessive, pulling back only makes it worse.
</Callout>

## Nose high and nose low

General techniques recommended by the industry (each manufacturer sets its own):

| Nose high, speed decreasing | Nose low, speed increasing |
|---|---|
| Autopilot and autothrust off | Autopilot and autothrust off |
| **Push** to unload the wing and lower the nose | If stalled, **recover from the stall first** |
| If needed, **bank** so the nose drops sooner | **Level the wings** the shortest way |
| Adjust thrust | **Pull** afterwards, without exceeding load limits |
| Level off as the nose approaches the horizon | Reduce thrust and use speed brakes if speed rises a lot |

Order matters: nose low with a lot of bank, pulling before levelling the wings only tightens the spiral.

## Prevention is better

- **Manage energy**: speed, height and thrust matched to what is coming.
- **Monitor** the automation and the modes on the FMA (Module 17).
- Avoid known areas of turbulence and wake.
- Cross-check **indications** at any doubt: if the airspeed makes no sense, fly known attitude and thrust.
