## What is read back

The crew must **read back** to ATC the safety-related parts of clearances and instructions. Under the European rules of the air, these are always read back:

- **route clearances**;
- clearances and instructions to **enter, land on, take off from, hold short of, cross, taxi on or backtrack** on any runway;
- the **runway in use**, **altimeter settings**, **SSR codes**, **new frequencies**, and **level, heading and speed** instructions;
- **transition levels**.

Other clearances, including conditional and taxi clearances, are read back or acknowledged in a way that makes clear they have been understood.

The <Term id="readback" /> ends with the **call sign**. And ATC must **listen** to the readback and correct any error: the *hearback*.

### Example

> **ATC**: "Iberia 3456, descend flight level eight zero, turn right heading two two zero, contact Madrid Approach one two seven decimal one."
> **Pilot**: "Descend flight level eight zero, right heading two two zero, one two seven decimal one, Iberia 3456."

## Conditional clearances

A <Term id="conditional-clearance" /> is given in this order: **call sign – condition – clearance – condition repeated**.

> **ATC**: "Iberia 3456, behind landing A320 on short final, line up and wait runway 36 left, behind."
> **Pilot**: "Behind landing A320, line up and wait runway 36 left, behind, Iberia 3456."

Before acting, the pilot must **see** the aircraft in the condition and be sure it is the right one. <Term id="line-up-and-wait" /> is never a take-off clearance.

## Number traps

- **"To" and "two"**: "climb to two thousand" can be heard as "climb two two thousand". That is why the phrase is "climb **flight level**…" or "climb **altitude**…" and "to" is not used before numbers.
- **FL100 and FL110**, **FL200 and FL210**: easily confused. Pronounce "one **zero zero**" clearly.
- **Heading and level**: "heading 080" versus "flight level 80"; the key word always comes first.
- **Similar call signs** on the same frequency: when in doubt, **ask**; never accept an instruction that may be for someone else.

<Callout type="caution" label="If in doubt, ask">
No readback replaces understanding the instruction. "Say again" or "Confirm…" cost a few seconds; a wrong level can cause a loss of separation.
</Callout>
