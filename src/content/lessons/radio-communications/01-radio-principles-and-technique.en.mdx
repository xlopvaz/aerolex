## Aeronautical VHF

Voice communications between pilots and ATC use <Term id="vhf" /> between **118.000 and 136.975 MHz**. The international emergency frequency is **121.5 MHz**.

VHF travels **in straight lines**: it does not follow the Earth's curvature or pass through mountains. Its approximate range is:

> range (NM) ≈ 1.23 × √(height in ft)

At 10,000 ft, about **123 NM**; on the ground, at a few feet, only a few miles. If both stations are high up, the ranges of each add together.

In Europe, channels are spaced <Term id="channel-spacing" /> apart instead of 25 kHz, tripling the available frequencies. That is why there are frequencies such as **132.005**: on the radio all six digits are spoken.

## Call signs

- Airline aircraft: telephony designator + flight number, for example **IBERIA THREE FOUR FIVE SIX**. They are not abbreviated.
- Registrations: spelled with the <Term id="phonetic-alphabet" /> (**ECHO CHARLIE ALFA BRAVO CHARLIE**). Once contact is established, and only if ATC does so first, they can be shortened to the first letter and the last two: **ECHO BRAVO CHARLIE**.

## How numbers are spoken

They are transmitted **digit by digit**, except whole thousands and hundreds:

| Item | Spoken as |
|---|---|
| Level FL100 | "flight level **one zero zero**" |
| Altitude 2,500 ft | "**two thousand five hundred** feet" |
| Heading 080 | "heading **zero eight zero**" |
| QNH 1013 | "QNH **one zero one three**" |
| Frequency 118.7 | "**one one eight decimal seven**" |
| Code 7421 | "squawk **seven four two one**" |
| Wind 270/15 | "wind **two seven zero** degrees **one five** knots" |

In ICAO English some digits have a special pronunciation so they are not confused: **TREE** (3), **FOW-er** (4), **FIFE** (5), **NIN-er** (9) and **TOU-SAND** (thousand).

## Standard words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| <Term id="affirm" /> | Yes |
| <Term id="negative" /> | No, or permission not granted, or that is not correct |
| <Term id="roger" /> | I have received all of your last transmission |
| <Term id="wilco" /> | Understood and will comply |
| <Term id="say-again" /> | Repeat |
| <Term id="standby" /> | Wait and I will call you (not an approval) |
| <Term id="unable" /> | I cannot comply |
| **CORRECTION** | I made an error; the correct version is… |
| **BREAK** | Separates parts of a message |
| **READ BACK** | Repeat this message back to me exactly as received |

**ROGER** never answers a question needing yes or no, nor replaces a required readback.

## Technique

1. **Listen** before transmitting so you don't block anyone.
2. **Think** the message through before pressing.
3. Press, wait half a second and speak at an **even** pace, without fillers.
4. **Release** the switch when done: a stuck microphone leaves everyone without the frequency.

<Callout type="tip" label="Less is more">
A good message is short and in order: who you call, who you are, where, what. "Madrid Approach, Iberia 3456, FL120 descending FL80, information Bravo."
</Callout>
