## Distress and urgency

| Level | Word | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Distress** | <Term id="mayday" /> ×3 | Grave and imminent danger requiring immediate assistance |
| **Urgency** | <Term id="pan-pan" /> ×3 | A situation affecting the safety of the aircraft or someone on board, not requiring immediate assistance |

The message is sent on the frequency in use; if there is no answer, on **121.5 MHz**. As far as possible, it contains:

1. **MAYDAY, MAYDAY, MAYDAY** (or PAN PAN ×3);
2. the station being called;
3. the **call sign**;
4. the **nature** of the emergency;
5. the commander's **intentions**;
6. **position, level and heading**;
7. any other useful information (persons on board, fuel).

> "MAYDAY, MAYDAY, MAYDAY, Barcelona Control, Iberia 3456, engine fire, descending to FL100, diverting to Valencia, position 30 miles south of REUS, heading 200, 180 persons on board."

The station receiving the MAYDAY controls the distress traffic and can impose silence: "**STOP TRANSMITTING, MAYDAY**". When it is over: "**DISTRESS TRAFFIC ENDED**". An emergency can be **cancelled** if the situation is resolved.

## Emergency transponder codes

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **7700** | Emergency |
| **7600** | Radio communication failure |
| **7500** | Unlawful interference |

A memory aid: *seventy-five, taken alive*; *seventy-six, radio fix*; *seventy-seven, going to heaven*. If ATC has already assigned a code, it is normally kept.

## Radio communication failure

With an <Term id="rcf" />, first check the obvious: volume, frequency, headset, the other radio. If it persists:

**In VMC**, continue in visual conditions, **land at the nearest suitable aerodrome** and report the arrival to ATC by the quickest means.

**In IMC**, or if VMC cannot be maintained, the IFR flight:

- selects **7600**;
- maintains the last assigned level and speed, or the minimum flight altitude if higher, for a set time: under European rules, since May 2025, **20 minutes** (previously 7 in radar-surveillance airspace);
- then adjusts level and speed to the **flight plan** and follows the route to the destination navaid;
- starts the descent and approach as close as possible to the expected time, and lands if possible within the following **30 minutes**.

The same reform created code **7601** for an IFR flight that, without radio and in VMC, decides to land at the nearest suitable aerodrome. Not all States apply it yet: check the AIP and NOTAMs.

<Callout type="regional-diff" label="Each State may have differences">
Radio failure procedures are published in each country's AIP, which may differ from ICAO and the European rules. In flight, the current AIP is what counts.
</Callout>
