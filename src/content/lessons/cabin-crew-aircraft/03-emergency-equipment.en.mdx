<Callout type="official-procedure" label="The operator's manual prevails">
The type, quantity and location of each item depend on the aircraft and the operator, and are shown in the emergency equipment list in its manual.
</Callout>

## Four families of equipment

| Family | Typical items | What for |
|---|---|---|
| **Fire and smoke** | Portable fire extinguishers, <Term id="pbe">protective breathing equipment (PBE)</Term>, fire-resistant gloves | Fighting a fire in the cabin, a galley or a lavatory without breathing the smoke |
| **Oxygen** | Passenger masks (in the <Term id="psu" /> or seat back), portable bottles | A decompression, or a passenger who needs oxygen |
| **First aid** | First-aid kits, an emergency medical kit for use by medical professionals, on many aircraft a defibrillator | Looking after ill passengers or crew |
| **Evacuation and survival** | <Term id="evacuation-slide">Slides</Term>, torches, megaphones, life jackets (adult, child and infant), life rafts and an emergency locator transmitter on flights over water | Getting out of the aircraft and surviving until rescue |

European rules (Regulation (EU) No 965/2012) set which equipment is mandatory and, for some items, how many depending on the aircraft's number of seats. The operator may carry more.

## The pre-flight check

Before every flight, each cabin crew member checks the equipment in **their zone** using the operator's list. In general they verify that:

- each item is **in place** and accessible;
- **seals** are intact and indicators (for example, the gauge on an extinguisher or oxygen bottle) are in the correct range;
- nothing blocks access to the equipment or to the exits.

Any problem is reported to the senior cabin crew member, who informs the commander. Some missing or faulty items can prevent the flight from departing or limit the number of passengers, according to the operator's minimum equipment list.

<Callout type="tip" label="Know where it is">
In an emergency there is no time to search. That is why training puts so much weight on knowing where the equipment is on each aircraft type and on practising its use. The module assessment is at the end of this lesson.
</Callout>
