<Callout type="official-procedure" label="The operator's manual prevails">
Each airline configures its cabins its own way. The layout, door designations and crew positions for each aircraft are in the operator's manual.
</Callout>

## Single aisle and twin aisle

- **Single aisle** (narrowbody): one central aisle with seats on both sides, as on the A320 family or the Boeing 737. Typical for short and medium-haul flights.
- **Twin aisle** (widebody): two aisles, as on the A350 or the Boeing 787. They carry more passengers and, on long-haul flights, have crew rest areas.

The cabin is usually divided into **classes** (for example business and economy) and, for the crew, into **zones of responsibility** linked to each pair of doors.

## What is in a cabin

| Item | What it is | For cabin crew |
|---|---|---|
| **Galley** | The "kitchen": ovens, coffee makers, carts and compartments | Everything must be **latched** for take-off, landing and turbulence: a loose cart is a projectile |
| **Lavatories** | Toilets with a smoke detector and an automatic extinguisher in the waste bin | No smoking or tampering with the detector; checked before take-off and landing |
| <Term id="psu" /> | Panel above each row: lights, call button, air, signs and often oxygen masks | Answer calls and know where the masks drop |
| **Overhead bins** | For cabin baggage | Closed and not overloaded; never for items that cannot be stowed safely |
| **Crew seats** | Fold-down jump seats next to the doors, with a harness | Each crew member sits in theirs for take-off and landing, near their exit |
| **Interphone and PA** | Communication with the flight deck and passengers | Use the operator's call procedures |

## Doors with names

To talk about each door without confusion, many operators designate them by side and order from the nose: **L** (left) and **R** (right) followed by a number, so **L1** is the first door on the left and **R2** the second on the right. Check your aircraft's manual for how its doors are designated.

<Callout type="info" label="The cabin is safety equipment too">
Seats, seat belts, floor emergency lighting and exit signs are part of the aircraft's certification. Looking after them and checking they work is part of a cabin crew member's job.
</Callout>
