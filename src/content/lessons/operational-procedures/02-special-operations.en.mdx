## Low-visibility operations

Since the European all-weather operations reform (applicable from 2022), <Term id="lvo" /> are approaches or take-offs with an <Term id="rvr" /> **below 550 m** or a decision height **below 200 ft**.

| Category | Decision height | RVR |
|---|---|---|
| **CAT I** | 200 ft or more | 550 m or more (or 800 m visibility) |
| **CAT II** | 100 to below 200 ft | 300 m or more |
| **CAT III** | Below 100 ft or no DH | Below 300 m |

European rules **no longer subdivide** CAT III into IIIA and IIIB: the actual minima depend on the aircraft, its equipment and the operator's approval, with a minimum RVR of **75 m**. Some manuals still use the old terms.

For **take-off**, an RVR below 550 m is already a low-visibility take-off, and below **400 m** the operator needs a **specific approval**.

All this requires: a certified aircraft, a trained and current crew, and an aerodrome with active <Term id="lvp" /> protecting the ILS signals and the manoeuvring area.

## RVSM

In <Term id="rvsm" /> airspace, between **FL290 and FL410**, vertical separation is reduced from 2,000 to **1,000 ft**. To fly in it, the aircraft needs:

- two **independent** altitude measurement systems;
- an **altitude alerting** system;
- an **autopilot** with altitude hold;
- an altitude-reporting **transponder**.

If any of them fails, ATC is told, and may take the aircraft out of RVSM or give it 2,000 ft separation.

## ETOPS

Twins that fly further from an adequate aerodrome than a set one-engine flying time (generally **60 minutes**) need an <Term id="etops" /> approval. It sets the maximum diversion time (for example 120, 180 or more minutes) and requires engine reliability, specific maintenance, en-route alternates with suitable weather and fuel for the critical scenario: an engine failure or depressurisation at the worst point.

## Winter operations

The **clean aircraft concept** applies: no take-off with ice, frost or snow adhering to the wings, control surfaces, propellers or engine inlets. A few millimetres of rough frost are enough to cut lift significantly and raise the stall speed.

<Term id="de-icing" /> is done with fluids:

| Type | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| **I** | Thin, applied hot; removes ice but protects only briefly |
| **II and IV** | Thickened; stay on the wing and protect longer; shear off with speed on take-off |
| **III** | Thickened for aircraft with lower rotation speeds |

The <Term id="holdover-time" /> starts at the **beginning of the final application** and depends on the fluid, temperature and the type and intensity of precipitation. If it is exceeded or in doubt, the aircraft is inspected or treated again.

<Callout type="caution" label="The tables are an estimate">
Holdover times are guidance: heavier precipitation, wind or jet blast can shorten them. The final decision on whether the aircraft is clean is the commander's.
</Callout>
