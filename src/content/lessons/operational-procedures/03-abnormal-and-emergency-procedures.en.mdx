## The philosophy

With any failure, the order is the same: **aviate, navigate, communicate**. Then the problem is identified, memory items are carried out if any, the **checklist** (<Term id="qrh" /> or electronic alerts) is applied, and a decision is made: continue, return or divert.

## Depressurisation and emergency descent

After a <Term id="decompression" /> at high altitude, the time of useful consciousness is seconds. The basic actions of an <Term id="emergency-descent" /> are:

1. **Oxygen masks** on and communication between the pilots established.
2. Start the **descent**: idle thrust, speed brakes, the speed set by the manufacturer. Under European rules, the crew navigates as they see fit, taking terrain and traffic into account.
3. **Warn**: MAYDAY to ATC and transponder **7700** unless ATC has assigned another code; warn the cabin.
4. Level off at **10,000 ft** or the minimum safe altitude if higher.

The European rules reformed in 2025 added that ATC first broadcasts an emergency message to other traffic, which keeps its clearance and watches visually and with TCAS.

## Wind shear

<Term id="wind-shear" /> near the ground, especially in thunderstorm microbursts, can take away speed and height in seconds. The best defence is to **avoid** it: no take-off or landing with thunderstorms nearby or wind shear warnings.

If the warning goes off:

- **maximum thrust**;
- pitch attitude per the manufacturer's procedure, following the flight guidance, close to the stall warning if needed;
- **don't change configuration** (flaps, gear) until clear;
- report to ATC afterwards.

## Smoke and fire

Fire on board is one of the most critical emergencies: it can become uncontrollable within minutes. Therefore:

- oxygen masks and smoke goggles;
- apply the smoke or fire checklist and try to **isolate the source**;
- if the smoke or fire is not controlled quickly, **land as soon as possible** at the nearest suitable aerodrome, without waiting to understand the whole problem.

Lithium batteries in the cabin are handled with the cabin procedure (water or non-flammable liquid after extinguishing; see the Cabin Crew Academy).

## Bird strike

<Term id="bird-strike" />s are most frequent at low level, on take-off and approach. If an engine is affected, engine failure procedures are applied and the aircraft returns or diverts. A strike on the windscreen or radome is also assessed. All are **reported**.

## Overweight landing and fuel jettison

If the aircraft has to return soon after take-off, it may be heavier than the maximum landing mass. The options:

- **burn fuel** in a hold, if the emergency allows;
- **jettison fuel** (<Term id="fuel-jettison" />), if the aircraft has the system, in areas and at heights given by ATC;
- **land overweight**, if urgency demands; maintenance then carries out a special inspection.

<Callout type="official-procedure" label="The manufacturer's and operator's manual prevails">
The specific actions, speeds and attitudes are set by the manufacturer and the operator for each type. This lesson explains the logic; it does not replace the QRH or the operations manual.
</Callout>
