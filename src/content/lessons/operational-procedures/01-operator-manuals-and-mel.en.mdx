## The operator

To carry out commercial air transport in the EU, a company needs an <Term id="aoc" />, issued by its national authority, with **operations specifications** stating which aircraft, which areas and which special operations it may conduct (low visibility, ETOPS, RVSM, dangerous goods…).

## The operations manual

Everything the crew must do is in the <Term id="operations-manual" />. In the EU it usually has four parts:

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| **A** | General: organisation, responsibilities, flight time limitations, fuel policy, minima, general procedures |
| **B** | Aircraft type: limitations, normal, abnormal and emergency procedures, performance, mass and balance, MEL |
| **C** | Routes, regions and aerodromes: minima, procedures and information for each place |
| **D** | Training: training and checking programmes |

The aircraft <Term id="sop" /> are in Part B. The crew must follow the manual; if a situation is not covered, the **commander** decides. The commander is responsible for the safety of the aircraft, the people and the cargo, and has authority to give all necessary orders.

## Documents on board

Among others, European rules require carrying: the aircraft flight manual or equivalent, the **registration** and **airworthiness** certificates (originals), the **noise** certificate, a copy of the **AOC**, the **radio** licence, the **insurance** certificate, the **journey log** or equivalent, the **operational flight plan**, **NOTAM and weather** information, the **mass and balance** documentation and the relevant parts of the operations manual.

## Flying with inoperative equipment: the MEL

No aircraft always flies with everything working. The rules allow it in a controlled way:

- The <Term id="mmel" /> is approved by the authority for each aircraft **type**: it lists what may be inoperative at the start of a flight and under what conditions.
- The <Term id="mel" /> is **each operator's** version, adapted to its aircraft and operations. It can never be **less restrictive** than the MMEL.
- Many items carry procedures: **(M)** for maintenance, for example locking a valve, and **(O)** for the crew, for example not flying into forecast icing.
- The <Term id="cdl" /> covers missing **external parts** (a panel, a fairing), normally with a performance penalty.

### Rectification intervals

Each item has a <Term id="rectification-interval" />:

| Category | Interval |
|---|---|
| **A** | As stated in the MEL itself |
| **B** | 3 calendar days |
| **C** | 10 calendar days |
| **D** | 120 calendar days |

Days are counted **excluding the day the defect is discovered**. Categories B, C and D may be extended **once** under an approved procedure; A never.

<Callout type="caution" label="If it is not in the MEL, it does not fly">
If an inoperative item does not appear in the MEL, or its conditions are not met, the aircraft cannot depart until it is repaired.
</Callout>
