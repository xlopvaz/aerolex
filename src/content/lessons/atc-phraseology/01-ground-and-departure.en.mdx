## The typical sequence

The examples are in English, the language of international radio; Spanish phraseology follows the same structure.

### 1. ATIS

Before calling, listen to the <Term id="atis" />, identified by a letter. When calling, say you have it: "**information Bravo**".

### 2. Departure clearance

> **Pilot**: "Madrid Delivery, Iberia 3456, stand 24, request clearance to Paris, information Bravo."
> **ATC**: "Iberia 3456, cleared to Paris, NANDO 1N departure, climb flight level 130, squawk 4721."
> **Pilot**: "Cleared to Paris, NANDO 1N departure, climb flight level 130, squawk 4721, Iberia 3456."

A route <Term id="clearance" /> **does not authorise take-off or entering a runway**.

### 3. Start-up and pushback

> **Pilot**: "Iberia 3456, stand 24, request start-up and push-back."
> **ATC**: "Iberia 3456, start-up and push-back approved, facing south."

### 4. Taxi

> **ATC**: "Iberia 3456, taxi to holding point runway 36 left via Alfa, Bravo, hold short of runway 32 right."
> **Pilot**: "Taxi to holding point runway 36 left via Alfa, Bravo, hold short of runway 32 right, Iberia 3456."

**Hold short** instructions and **runway crossings** are always read back. If unsure of the route, ask for "**progressive taxi**" or ask.

### 5. Entering the runway and take-off

> **ATC**: "Iberia 3456, line up and wait runway 36 left."
> **ATC**: "Iberia 3456, wind 340 degrees 8 knots, runway 36 left, **cleared for take-off**."
> **Pilot**: "Runway 36 left, cleared for take-off, Iberia 3456."

The word **"take-off"** is used **only** when clearing for take-off or cancelling it. At any other time the word is "departure". So a phrase like "ready for take-off" becomes "**ready for departure**" or simply "**ready**".

### 6. When you have to stop

| Situation | ATC | Pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Not yet rolling | "Hold position, cancel take-off, I say again, cancel take-off" | "Holding" |
| Take-off roll started | "**Stop immediately**, I say again, stop immediately" | "**Stopping**" |
| Pilot rejects on their own | — | "Stopping" |

<Callout type="caution" label="Runway incursions">
Many runway incursions start with a misheard or wrongly read back "hold short". At the slightest doubt on the ground, stop and ask.
</Callout>
