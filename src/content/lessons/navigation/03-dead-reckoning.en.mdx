## Dead reckoning and pilotage

The FAA PHAK distinguishes two basic ways of navigating without radio aids:

- **Pilotage**: following visible references on the ground, such as rivers, roads or towns.
- <Term id="dead-reckoning" />: working out position from a known point using heading, speed, wind and time.

In practice they are combined: dead reckoning tells you **where you should be** and pilotage confirms **where you are**.

## Time, speed and distance

Everything comes down to **distance = speed × time**:

- 84 NM at a groundspeed of 126 kt → 84 / 126 = 0.67 h = **40 minutes**.
- If the aircraft burns 30 litres an hour, in those 40 minutes it will use **20 litres**.

A useful trick: at 120 kt you cover **2 NM a minute**; at 180 kt, 3 NM a minute.

## The navigation log

Before the flight a **navigation log** is prepared with each leg: track, distance, forecast wind, heading, groundspeed, time and fuel. In flight, the **actual time** over each checkpoint is written down and compared with the planned one. If you are early or late, the real groundspeed differs from the plan: the wind is not the forecast one, and the following estimates are corrected.

## The 1-in-60 rule

An error of **1°** held for **60 NM** moves the aircraft about **1 NM** sideways. Hence:

> track error (°) ≈ distance off track (NM) × 60 / distance flown (NM)

and, to go straight to the destination,

> closing angle (°) ≈ distance off track (NM) × 60 / distance to go (NM)

**The total correction to reach the destination is the sum of the two.**

Example: after **30 NM**, you find yourself **3 NM right** of track with **60 NM** to go.

1. Track error ≈ 3 × 60 / 30 = **6°**.
2. Closing angle ≈ 3 × 60 / 60 = **3°**.
3. Turn **9° left** to head straight for the destination.

If instead you want to **regain the track** in the same distance you have flown, you correct by **double** the error (12° in the example) and, once back on track, keep only the error correction (6°).

## If you get lost

The principles are simple:

- **Don't panic** and keep flying the aircraft on a steady heading.
- **Climb** if it is safe, to see further and improve radio reception.
- Use every means available: **radio aids**, **GNSS** and **ask ATC for help**, as they can locate the aircraft.
- Keep the **fuel** in mind and don't wait too long to ask for help.
