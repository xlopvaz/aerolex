## Heading and track are not the same

- <Term id="track" /> is the aircraft's path **over the ground**.
- <Term id="heading" /> is where the **nose points**.

With no wind they coincide. With a crosswind the aircraft is pushed sideways: **drift** is the angle between heading and track. To follow the desired track, the pilot points the nose slightly into the wind: this is the <Term id="wind-correction-angle" />.

## The wind triangle

Three vectors form a triangle:

1. The **aircraft through the air**: heading and TAS.
2. The **wind**: the direction it blows **from** and its speed.
3. The **result over the ground**: track and <Term id="ground-speed">groundspeed</Term>.

It used to be solved by drawing it or with an E6B-type flight computer. Today the FMS calculates it, but understanding it lets you check that the result makes sense.

<Callout type="info" label="True or magnetic?">
Forecast winds are given relative to **true** north. Winds given by the tower or ATIS for take-off and landing are relative to **magnetic** north, like the runways.
</Callout>

## Wind components

If the angle between the wind and the track is α:

- **headwind (or tailwind) component** = wind × cos α;
- **crosswind component** = wind × sin α.

Values worth remembering:

| α | sin α | cos α |
|---|---|---|
| 30° | 0.5 | ≈ 0.9 |
| 45° | ≈ 0.7 | ≈ 0.7 |
| 60° | ≈ 0.9 | 0.5 |
| 90° | 1 | 0 |

Example: a 20 kt wind at 60° to the track → about **18 kt of crosswind** and **10 kt of headwind**.

## Working out drift in your head

A useful approximation: **maximum drift** (with a full crosswind) is

> maximum drift (°) ≈ wind (kt) × 60 / TAS (kt)

and for other angles you multiply by sin α.

Full example: true track **090°**, TAS **120 kt**, wind **360/20** (from the north):

1. The wind is a full crosswind from the left: α = 90°.
2. Maximum drift ≈ 20 × 60 / 120 = **10°**.
3. Correct 10° into the wind: true heading **080°**.
4. With no headwind component, groundspeed is almost the TAS: about **118 kt**.

## From true to compass

The complete chain to get the heading read on the compass is:

**true track → (wind correction) → true heading → (variation) → magnetic heading → (deviation) → compass heading**

Remember from the instruments module: **east** variation is **subtracted** and **west** is **added**.
