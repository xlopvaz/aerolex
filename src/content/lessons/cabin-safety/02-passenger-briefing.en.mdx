<Callout type="official-procedure" label="The operator's manual prevails">
The wording of the demonstration, the announcements and the safety card are specific to each airline and each aircraft type.
</Callout>

## Before take-off

Under EASA's acceptable means of compliance (AMC1 CAT.OP.MPA.170), the <Term id="safety-demonstration" /> must brief passengers, where applicable, on:

1. the use of **safety belts**, including how to fasten and unfasten them;
2. the location of the **emergency exits**;
3. the location and use of **oxygen equipment**, including the instruction to put out any smoking materials when oxygen is in use;
4. the location and use of **life jackets**, if the flight requires them.

It is completed by the **safety card** at each seat, preferably pictographic, which shows among other things the exits and how they open, the <Term id="brace-position" /> and the use of oxygen and life jackets. EASA recommends a specific card for people seated at an exit.

## Seats next to exits

People seated in rows with direct access to an exit may have to **open it** or help in an evacuation. That is why the operator must allocate those seats so that the passengers seated there do not impede an evacuation. Normally they do **not** include, for example:

- people with reduced mobility or who need help to evacuate;
- children and passengers travelling with infants;
- people who cannot understand the crew's instructions or do not want that role.

Anyone seated at an exit gets an **additional briefing** on what to do.

## Special categories of passengers

The regulation uses this term for people who need **special conditions, assistance or devices** on board — for example people with reduced mobility, infants and unaccompanied children, passengers with medical needs, or deportees and people in custody. The operator sets how they are carried and the crew receive that information before the flight.

<Callout type="tip" label="Your own mask first">
In a decompression, an adult travelling with a child or someone who needs help **puts on their own mask first** and then helps. It is one of the key messages of the safety briefing.
</Callout>
