## The speed of sound and Mach number

The speed of sound in air depends **only on temperature**: the colder it is, the slower. In ISA it is about **661 kt** at sea level (15 °C) and about **574 kt** at the tropopause (−56.5 °C).

<Term id="mach-number">Mach number</Term> is the ratio of <Term id="tas">true airspeed</Term> to the local speed of sound. Flying at Mach 0.80 means flying at 80 % of the speed of sound in that air.

Because the speed of sound falls with altitude while, at a constant <Term id="ias" />, TAS rises, climbing at constant indicated airspeed makes the Mach number **increase**. That is why jets climb at a fixed IAS and, above a certain altitude, switch to a fixed Mach number.

## The flight regimes

| Regime | Approximate Mach number |
|---|---|
| Subsonic | Below 0.75 |
| Transonic | 0.75 to 1.20 |
| Supersonic | 1.20 to 5.00 |
| Hypersonic | Above 5.00 |

## Critical Mach

Air speeds up over the top of the wing. So even when the aircraft is flying below Mach 1, the flow somewhere on the wing can reach the speed of sound. The aircraft Mach number at which this first happens is the **critical Mach number** (M<sub>CRIT</sub>).

Above it, **shock waves** form on the wing, which:

- sharply increase drag (**wave drag**);
- can make the flow separate behind them and cause high-speed **buffet**;
- move the centre of pressure aft, which can make the aircraft pitch nose-down (**Mach tuck**).

## Why swept wings

On a swept wing, only the component of the airflow perpendicular to the leading edge "counts" for compressibility. As a result, **critical Mach rises** and the aircraft can fly faster before shock-wave problems appear.

In exchange, sweep has low-speed drawbacks: a tendency to tip stall, less lift (hence the large flaps and slats on jets) and a tendency to Dutch roll, which jets correct with yaw dampers.

<Callout type="info" label="Speed limits">
Jets have two limits: a maximum operating IAS (V<sub>MO</sub>) at lower altitudes and a maximum operating Mach number (M<sub>MO</sub>) high up. Each aircraft's values are in its manual.
</Callout>
