## The basic measurements of a wing

- **Span:** the distance from one tip of the <Term id="wing" /> to the other.
- **Chord:** the distance between the leading and trailing edges of an <Term id="airfoil" />. On most wings it changes from root to tip.
- **Wing area:** the area of the wing seen from above.

## Five design choices

| Feature | What it is | What it achieves |
|---|---|---|
| <Term id="aspect-ratio">Aspect ratio</Term> | Span² / area | High: less <Term id="induced-drag" /> (gliders, long-haul jets). Low: stronger, more manoeuvrable wings |
| **Taper** | Tip chord / root chord | Better lift distribution and a lighter structure, but it can make the tip stall first |
| **Sweep** | The wing angled backwards | Delays compressibility effects at high speed and adds lateral stability |
| **Dihedral** | The upward angle of the wings from horizontal | Lateral stability: the aircraft tends to level its wings |
| **Washout** | Lower angle of incidence at the tip than at the root | The root stalls before the tip, so the ailerons stay effective |

## Why it matters where the stall starts

The ailerons are near the tips. If the stall started there, the pilot would lose roll control just when it is most needed, and a wing could drop sharply. That is why designers want the stall to begin **at the root** and spread outwards, using geometric or aerodynamic washout or stall strips on the leading edge.

Swept wings naturally tend to stall at the tips, because of airflow moving outwards along the span. Swept-wing aircraft use devices to correct this, such as wing fences or vortex generators.

## Wingtip devices

<Term id="winglet">Winglets</Term> and other wingtip devices weaken the wingtip vortices and, with them, induced drag. The result is lower fuel burn, especially on long flights, at the cost of some extra weight and load on the wing structure.

<Callout type="info" label="Something to look for">
Look at an airliner's wing from the cabin window: you will see the sweep, the taper towards the tip, the dihedral (the wings rise slightly) and, on many models, a device at the tip.
</Callout>
