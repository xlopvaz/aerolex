## Why they are needed

A wing designed to fly fast with little drag in cruise produces little lift at low speed. To take off and land on runways of reasonable length, the wing has to **change shape**: increase its camber and sometimes its area. That is what high-lift devices do.

## Trailing-edge flaps

| Type | How it works | Characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| **Plain** | The rear of the wing hinges downward | Increases camber; simple, but quite draggy |
| **Split** | Only a plate on the lower surface drops | High drag from the turbulent wake behind it |
| **Slotted** | When lowered, it opens a slot that lets high-pressure air flow to the upper surface | Delays separation: more lift with less drag |
| **Fowler** | Moves rearward and then down, often with one or more slots | Increases camber **and** wing area: the most effective, common on airliners |

<Term id="flaps">Flaps</Term> increase C<sub>L</sub> max, which **reduces stall speed**. In return they add drag and, in general, the <Term id="stall" /> occurs at a slightly **lower** <Term id="angle-of-attack" /> than with a clean wing. The first flap settings mainly add lift; the last ones mainly add drag, useful for steeper approaches.

## Leading-edge devices

- **Fixed slots:** ducts in the leading edge that lead air from the lower to the upper surface.
- <Term id="slats">Slats</Term>: movable leading-edge sections that move forward and open a slot.
- **Krueger flaps:** panels that fold out from the underside of the leading edge.

All of them energise the boundary layer on the upper surface and **delay separation**, so the wing can reach a **higher critical angle of attack** before it stalls.

<Term id="spoilers">Spoilers</Term> are not high-lift devices, but they complete the system: they dump lift when the crew wants to descend, slow down or keep the aircraft on the runway after landing.

<Callout type="caution" label="Maximum speeds">
Each flap setting has a maximum extended speed (V<sub>FE</sub>), because the loads on the devices grow with speed. Each aircraft's values are in its manual.
</Callout>
