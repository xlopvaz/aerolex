<Callout type="official-procedure" label="The operator's manual prevails">
Airlines may apply stricter rules than the general ones, and the procedures for a dangerous goods incident are those in their manual and approved training.
</Callout>

## Lithium batteries

They are by far the most common dangerous goods on board. The general rules, as summarised by the FAA from the international rules, depend on **capacity in watt-hours (Wh)**:

| Capacity | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Up to 100 Wh** | Allowed |
| **101 to 160 Wh** | Only with **airline approval**; maximum **2 spare batteries** per passenger |
| **Over 160 Wh** | **Forbidden** (except some wheelchairs and mobility aids) |

If the capacity is not shown, it is calculated: **Wh = mAh × V / 1,000**. A 20,000 mAh power bank at 3.7 V has 20,000 × 3.7 / 1,000 = **74 Wh**.

### Why spares go in the cabin

**Loose spare batteries** and **power banks** may only go in **cabin baggage**, with their terminals protected against short circuits. If one goes into <Term id="thermal-runaway" /> in the cabin, the crew can see it and fight it (Module 4). In the hold they cannot.

### Stricter airline rules

Airlines may be stricter. For example, since 15 January 2026 the Lufthansa Group does not allow power banks to be **used or charged** on board, limits them to **two** per person and does not allow them in the overhead bins, only under the seat, in the seat pocket or on the passenger. The group says the measure follows the recommendations of EASA, the FAA, IATA and ICAO. That is why it is always worth knowing your own operator's policy.

## Other common cases

According to the FAA's guidance for passengers:

- **E-cigarettes**: cabin baggage only, and they may not be charged on board.
- **Lighters and matches**: one on the person; torch-type lighters are forbidden.
- **Alcoholic drinks**: over 70 % alcohol, forbidden; between 24 % and 70 %, up to 5 litres per person in unopened retail packaging.
- **Dry ice** to keep food cold: up to 2.5 kg per person, with airline approval and in packaging that lets the gas out.

## Hidden dangerous goods

Many dangerous goods arrive undeclared, inside baggage with innocent descriptions. Some clues:

- **camping gear**: gas cylinders, fuel;
- **diving equipment**: compressed-air cylinders, high-power torches;
- **car or motorbike tools and parts**: wet batteries, fuel;
- **cleaning or pool products**: oxidisers and corrosives;
- **"personal effects"** of someone moving house.

If a crew member suspects that baggage contains forbidden dangerous goods, they report it according to the procedure. Cases of undeclared dangerous goods are reported to the authority.
