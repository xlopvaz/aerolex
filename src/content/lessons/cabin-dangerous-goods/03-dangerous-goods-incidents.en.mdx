<Callout type="official-procedure" label="The operator's manual prevails">
Airlines may apply stricter rules than the general ones, and the procedures for a dangerous goods incident are those in their manual and approved training.
</Callout>

## ICAO guidance

ICAO Document 9481, *Emergency Response Guidance*, contains checklists for crews facing dangerous goods incidents on board. Operators adapt them to their aircraft. They share some principles:

1. **Inform the commander** immediately.
2. **Identify** the item, asking the passenger if possible.
3. **Protect** people: move passengers away from the area and protect yourself.
4. **Contain** the problem.
5. **Monitor** the item for the rest of the flight.
6. **Inform** the ground so that the emergency services know what to expect.

## Spills and leaks

With a spilled liquid or a suspicious chemical smell:

- put on **gloves** (and, if there is smoke or fumes, the smoke hood);
- **move** passengers away;
- **absorb** the spill with the material in the kit or with blankets and towels;
- put the item and the contaminated material in plastic **bags**, tightly closed;
- stow it where there are no passengers, according to the procedure;
- **do not use water** on an unknown product without knowing what it is.

Many airlines carry a **spill response kit** with gloves, strong bags, absorbent material and ties.

## Fire and batteries

If the problem is a fire or a battery heating up, the Module 4 fire procedure applies: extinguisher, cooling with water or other non-alcoholic liquids and **not moving** the device while it is burning, smoking or very hot. FAA SAFO 09013 remains the reference.

## After landing

- The commander informs the ground **before landing**: what the item is, where it is and what has been done.
- The item is pointed out to ground staff or the fire service.
- The crew **writes a report**: dangerous goods incidents are reported to the authority.
