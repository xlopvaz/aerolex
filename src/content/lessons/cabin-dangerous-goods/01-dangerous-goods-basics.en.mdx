<Callout type="official-procedure" label="The operator's manual prevails">
Airlines may apply stricter rules than the general ones, and the procedures for a dangerous goods incident are those in their manual and approved training.
</Callout>

## What they are

<Term id="dangerous-goods" /> are articles or substances that can endanger health, safety, property or the environment. Many are everyday items: batteries, aerosols, perfumes, paints, camping gas, fireworks or dry ice.

The rules are:

- ICAO **Annex 18**, with the principles;
- the ICAO **Technical Instructions** (Doc 9284), with the details. They are mandatory;
- in practice, airlines work with the **IATA** regulations, based on the Technical Instructions.

Some goods are **forbidden** on any aircraft. Others are accepted as cargo only if properly **classified, packed, marked, labelled and documented**. Others, finally, may only travel on cargo aircraft.

## The nine classes

| Class | Hazard | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Explosives | Fireworks, ammunition |
| **2** | Gases | Aerosols, camping gas cylinders, fire extinguishers |
| **3** | Flammable liquids | Paints, petrol, some perfumes |
| **4** | Flammable solids | Matches, some metal powders |
| **5** | Oxidisers and organic peroxides | Some pool chemicals, concentrated bleach |
| **6** | Toxic and infectious substances | Pesticides, diagnostic specimens |
| **7** | Radioactive material | Medical isotopes |
| **8** | Corrosives | Acids, wet-cell batteries |
| **9** | Miscellaneous | **Lithium batteries**, dry ice, strong magnets |

Each class has a **diamond-shaped label** with its own colour and symbol. Cabin crew do not need to memorise them all, but they do need to recognise that a package with a hazard diamond **should not be in the cabin**.

## Informing the commander

When the aircraft carries dangerous goods as cargo, the commander receives the <Term id="notoc" /> in writing: what the goods are, how much and where they are loaded. That way, in an emergency, the crew knows what it is dealing with and can inform the emergency services.

## Training

Operating rules require **all crew** to be trained in dangerous goods, even at airlines that do not accept them as cargo. The reason is simple: dangerous goods come on board anyway, in passengers' baggage.
